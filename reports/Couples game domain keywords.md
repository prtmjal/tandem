# Own the brand, earn the keywords

The best domain for this game is a cheap, distinctive brand name on .com, not a keyword domain. The strongest candidate available on current evidence is **tandemafterdark.com**: no sign of registration or a conflicting brand turned up, it costs about **$11 a year**, it matches the name already in the product, and it scored **22 of 30** on the shortlist below, ahead of every exact-match option. The one condition is that "Tandem" clears a trademark search. The word is already registered in software and entertainment classes, and a children's app trades as Tandem at playtandem.com. The keyword domains are either taken or poor value. **couplesgames.com is listed at $4,195** and **couplegame.com shows a for-sale page with no price**. Google has said for years that keywords in a domain give no ranking bonus. Generic names cannot be registered as trademarks in the US or India, and in AI answers they collect no branded mentions. Keyword demand belongs on pages instead: /couples-games, /questions-for-couples, /truth-or-dare-for-couples, /long-distance-relationship-games and a few more, all on the brand root. Treat every number here with care. The Ahrefs plan refused every keyword, SERP and domain-rating call, and the session network blocked Google Trends, WHOIS/RDAP, NameBio and the candidate sites. So **no search volume in this report comes from a primary pull**, and **no domain's availability has been confirmed**. A short list of hand checks at the end must be done before any money is spent.

## Only two head terms have numbers, about 60,500 searches a month each

Real demand data is thin, and what exists comes second-hand. RankHero estimates **"couples games" at about 60,500 searches a month** (49,500 to 74,000 between Feb 2025 and Jan 2026), with **KD 34 and CPC $0.20** ([RankHero](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/couples-games)). It gives **"couple game" also about 60,500, with KD 38 and CPC $0.70** ([RankHero](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/couple-game)). Those two figures are the backbone of the demand case, and they come with a real doubt: RankHero calls itself an Etsy keyword tool ([RankHero](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/rankhero)). One research note treats the figures as Etsy data. The other points out that the identical 60,500 looks like a Google Keyword Planner bucket. That doubt is unresolved, so treat them as estimates of unknown origin. Even taken at face value, the CPCs say advertisers do not value these clicks highly. That matters when judging what a $4,195 keyword domain is really worth.

The long-tail figures are US-only and come from seodata.dev's related-keyword tables: **"conversation starters for couples" 9,900**, **"conversation questions for couples" 1,900** ([seodata.dev](https://www.seodata.dev/keyword/how-to-start-conversations-with-your-bf)) and **"get to know you questions for couples" 1,600 at a $0.11 CPC** ([seodata.dev](https://www.seodata.dev/keyword/how-to-get-to-know-someone-in-a-relationship)). The broadest adjacent term, **"date night ideas", ran 40,500 a month in the US, and "at home date night ideas" 6,600**, but those are 2024 figures ([QE Retail](https://www.qeretail.com/blog/seo-valentines-tips)). For the spicy cluster the only signal is indirect. Paired's top US keyword was "truth or dare questions spicy", bringing it about **2,400 visits a month** out of **roughly 119K total organic visits** in July 2025 ([Ahrefs public page](https://ahrefs.com/websites/paired.com)). That is traffic, not search volume. On Amazon US, "couple games" drew about **1,690 searches a week** in July 2026 ([AS Insight](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/couple-games)). That figure measures shopping, not Google.

Everything else is missing. There is no volume, KD or CPC for 23 of the 26 target keywords, including "questions for couples", "truth or dare for couples", "spicy questions for couples", "couples quiz", "long distance relationship games" and "couples app". No India or UK split exists for any term. No Hindi or Hinglish variant was measured. There is no Google Trends seasonality beyond a Semrush figure: US Valentine's-related searches roughly doubled, **from 17.2 million in January to 37.9 million in February** ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/news/380949-valentines-day-a-data-driven-love-story-of-trends-searches-and-online-dating/)). The singular "couple game" is common in Indian English, which makes it the natural India term. But no country split confirms this, and Indian results for "couple games" lean heavily toward wedding and sangeet games ([WeddingWire India](https://www.weddingwire.in/wedding-tips/indian-couple-wedding-games--c5565); [VenueLook](https://www.venuelook.com/blog/indian-wedding-games-list-fun-activities-for-mehndi-sangeet-haldi-and-reception-2026)). So a bare "couple game" domain would split its India traffic between two very different intents.

## Publishers own the question lists; the spicy cluster is fragmented

Who ranks matters more than the domain string. No DR, Google top-10 order or SERP features could be observed, because Ahrefs refused `serp-overview` and the search tool used was not Google. The pattern across result sets is still consistent. The **"questions for couples" and "deep questions" clusters are led only by large brands**: Bustle, BetterHelp, Marriage.com, The Knot, Calm and Psych Central, with no small or exact-match sites among them ([BetterHelp](https://www.betterhelp.com/advice/relations/learning-in-love-50-relationship-building-questions-for-couples); [The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/content/deep-questions-for-couples); [Calm](https://www.calm.com/blog/questions-to-ask-in-a-relationship)). This is the hardest cluster for a new site. The **"couples games" head term** mixes Paired's "best online games for couples" with a video-game publisher's listicle and a lot of spam on hijacked subdomains ([Paired](https://www.paired.com/articles/best-online-games-for-couples); [Plarium](https://plarium.com/en/blog/best-games-for-couples-online-in-2026-play-together-anywhere/)).

The **spicy questions, truth-or-dare and couple-quiz clusters look the most winnable**. Results there are split among small app blogs and keyword-led partial-match domains: quiz-couple.com, lovecouplez.com, pairplaycouples.app, privegame.com, tryamora.app and truthordareai.com, alongside Marriage.com, StyleCraze and Paired ([quiz-couple.com](https://quiz-couple.com/en/spicy-couple-quiz/); [lovecouplez.com](https://lovecouplez.com/spicy-intimate-questions-for-couples-in-love/); [truthordareai.com](https://www.truthordareai.com/); [Marriage.com](https://www.marriage.com/advice/relationship/truth-or-dare-questions-for-couples/)). For "truth or dare for couples spicy", about **10 of 20 results were App Store listings**, a sign of strong app intent ([App Store example](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id933524873)). Long-distance games belong to Paired, Marriage.com, TechCult and Bonobology, the Indian relationship publisher with a deep set of couple-game pages ([Paired](https://www.paired.com/articles/games-for-long-distance-couples); [Bonobology](https://www.bonobology.com/long-distance-relationship-games/)).

Three findings shape the domain decision. First, **no exact-match domain for any head term appeared in any result set**: not couplesgames.com, questionsforcouples.com or couplequiz.com. Second, Paired, Candle, Amora and Privé rank through question-list posts on brand domains. Paired is the main content competitor in almost every cluster. Third, the field already holds many close rivals on keyword .app and .com names: ourcouple.app, lovebae.app, couplesquiz.app, couplestruthdare.com, couplesuite.com and syncwithlove.com. The last of these already offers two-device play with no account ([syncwithlove.com](https://syncwithlove.com/); [lovebae.app](https://lovebae.app/games); [couplestruthdare.com](https://couplestruthdare.com/)). One more generic keyword name would be hard to tell apart from them.

## An exact-match domain buys a $4,195 name, not a ranking

Google's public position is plain and consistent. John Mueller: **"There's no secret (or public) SEO-bonus for having your keywords in the domain name"** ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/keyword-domains-seo)). He has also advised against a keyword-only domain for anything long-term ([SEJ 2024](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/john-mueller-on-keyword-domain-names-and-branding-for-seo/504423/)). Gary Illyes said keyword domains "don't really help much" and "can be helpful for CTR sometimes" ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-on-top-level-domains-for-seo-35752.html)). The 2012 EMD update demoted thin exact-match sites in **0.6% of English-US queries** ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-emd-update-15776.html)). Backlinko's 11.8-million-result study tied rankings to authority and referring domains, not keyword strings. That study is from about 2020 ([Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/search-engine-ranking)). The steelman case for an EMD is small: a possible CTR nudge, and keyword anchor text when people link using the bare name. Neither has been measured. No independent 2023-2026 study isolates EMD ranking effects.

AI search tilts further toward brands. Ahrefs' December 2025 analysis of 75,000 brands found **branded web mentions were the strongest predictor of AI citations**, and **76% of AI Overview citations come from pages already in Google's top 10** ([summary](https://myamazonguy.com/news/brand-mentions-in-ai-search-platforms/)). Semrush and Kevin Indig found **61.7% of AI citations are "ghost citations"** that link a page without naming the brand ([Growth Memo](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/the-ghost-citation-problem)). A mention of "couples games" cannot be credited to anyone. A mention of a coined name can.

Price confirms the point. HugeDomains asks **$4,195 for couplesgames.com**, or $174.79 a month for 24 months. Its comparable "night" names range from **$995 to $7,195** ([HugeDomains](https://www.hugedomains.com/domain_profile.cfm?d=couplesgames.com); [DataNight](https://www.hugedomains.com/domain_profile.cfm?d=datanight.com)). No public sale of any couples, questions, date-night or truth-or-dare keyword domain was found. For context only: one NameBio day averaged **$619 per sale**, and 2024's 144,700 reported sales totalled about **$185M**, with .com making up **74.4%** of the dollar volume ([DNForum](https://www.dnforum.com/threads/499-815-in-sales-on-august-1st-mynotes-ai-sold-for-35-000.637771/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_aftermarket)). Paying $4,195 for a name that ranks no better, cannot be a trademark, and sits on a $0.20-CPC keyword is a poor trade before launch.

## .com, no sexual words, and a crowded "Tandem" register

**TLD.** .com remains the trust and recall default. In GrowthBadger's survey of 1,500 people, **.com was recalled correctly 44% of the time against 33% for .co**, and .com was trusted most ([GrowthBadger](https://growthbadger.com/top-level-domains/)). Keyword TLDs give no ranking lift ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ignores-content-in-tlds-22236.html)). Renewal costs at low-cost registrars are about **$11/yr for .com** (Porkbun), **$14.20-$14.93 for .app**, **$30-$31.20 for .co** and **$5.80 for .co.in** ([dev.to](https://dev.to/paullovelace/4-domain-registrars-ranked-by-renewal-price-october-2026-4eo0); [Porkbun .app](https://porkbun.com/about/domain-cost-calculator/app.txt); [Porkbun .co](https://porkbun.com/about/domain-cost-calculator/co.txt); [domainoffer.net](https://domainoffer.net/tld/co.in/porkbun)). .game runs **about $309-$340 a year**, an old figure ([Porkbun](https://porkbun.com/products/domains)). Ten years of tandem.game renewals would cost about as much as buying couplesgames.com. .app is HSTS-preloaded ([EuroDNS](https://www.eurodns.com/blog/hsts-preload-list-site-security)). That costs nothing here, because the server already runs behind TLS on Render, Railway or Fly. Use .com as the canonical host and hold .app and .in as redirects. A .in primary would geotarget India and weaken the US. Plain .in pricing was not found.

**Sexual words.** "Sex", "dirty", "naughty" and, more mildly, "spicy" put risk on every review surface and buy no ranking. Stripe refuses "mature audience content… designed for the purpose of sexual gratification" ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-gb-ca/legal/restricted-businesses)). The Couple Pass therefore depends on the business URL reading as a relationship game. Google Ads bars sexual content from Display, AdMob and YouTube, and since March 2025 it has required certification for dating and companionship ads ([Google Ads](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6023699?hl=en); [Google Ads dating policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15327789?hl=en-AU)). Apple requires store metadata to suit a 4+ audience whatever the app's rating, and Google Play bans sexually suggestive metadata ([Phiture](https://phiture.com/asostack/app-store-and-play-store-guidelines/); [Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en)). Meta prohibits sexually explicit language in ads ([Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/adult-sexual-solicitation-and-sexually-explicit-language/)). SafeSearch filters at URL level ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-safe-search-filter-url-24090.html)). An explicit domain would also misdescribe a game whose content rule is "suggestive at most, never graphic". "After Dark" is evocative without being explicit, so it is more likely to pass these reviews than "spicy". That is a judgment, not a tested result.

**Trademarks.** Descriptive names fail in both of the main markets. The USPTO refuses "merely descriptive" marks under TMEP §1209 ([BitLaw](https://www.bitlaw.com/source/tmep/1209.html)), and India's Trade Marks Act s.9(1)(b) refuses marks that designate the kind or purpose of goods, unless distinctiveness has been acquired ([Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/in/column/analysis-of-section-9(1)(b)-of-the-trade-marks-act,-1999/view)). couplesgames.com, couplegame.com and couplegames.app all fail that test. "Tandem" has a different problem: it is already registered. Tandem Diabetes Care holds a **TANDEM word mark (2023) covering classes 9, 10, 41 and 44**. Class 41 covers entertainment ([markinton](https://markinton.com/trademark/tandem-97566932)). The Tandem language-exchange app (Tripod Technology GmbH, 10M+ downloads) holds **US Reg. #5592755 in classes 9, 38 and 42**, according to a secondary aggregator ([markinton](https://markinton.com/trademark/tandem-86620583); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=net.tandem)). **playtandem.com is a live AI story app for children aged 2-8**, published by Early Ideas Ltd ([playtandem.com](https://www.playtandem.com/)). Sharing a name with a kids' brand is both a confusion risk and a brand-safety risk for an 18+ game. A 2021 puzzle game also used the name ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tandem:_A_Tale_of_Shadows)). "Tandem After Dark" as a compound is easier to tell apart than tandemgame.app or tandem.game. A search for the phrase found no conflicting brand ([search result](https://www.sportskeeda.com/us/reality-tv/sneaky-links-dating-after-dark-episode-2-recap-an-activity-designed-test-connections-prompts-shifting-dynamics)). "After Dark" alone, though, is a common phrase ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/After_Dark)). No official USPTO, EUIPO, UKIPO or IP India search was run.

Those are exactly the criteria that compound over years, while search demand on the domain string earns nothing.

**Final pick: tandemafterdark.com as the canonical domain**, with tandemafterdark.app and a .in or .co.in held as 301 redirects, all for under $35 a year. This pick holds only if a clearance search for TANDEM AFTER DARK in classes 9, 28 and 41 comes back usable in the US, EU, UK and India. If it does not, move to a coined name in the bothsaidyes.app mould, ideally on .com. Do not fall back to a keyword domain. One tension with the earlier market research needs naming. That report suggested keeping "After Dark" as a level band rather than the public brand, so that review teams see a softer name. The evidence here supports keeping it in the domain on balance: it is not explicit, it makes "Tandem" far more distinguishable, and it signals 18+ without a sexual word. That is a judgment call. App store titles should still follow the safe pattern ("Name: Couples Game for Two Phones") to meet the 4+ metadata rule.

## Hybrid strategy: brand root, keyword pages, one cluster each

Put the keyword work on paths of the brand root, as the earlier growth research advised. Do not use separate EMD microsites. Every page should be a short list with five to ten playable cards and a "play this on two phones" button that opens a room. The repo already sets a constraint that shapes this. The landing page may show **only real Level 1-3 cards** (`tests/landing.test.ts`), loads no third-party requests, and makes only privacy claims the code backs. Extend that test to every keyword page. The "spicy" pages then sell the climb without printing later-level text, which also keeps them SafeSearch- and ad-review-safe.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the usual intuition about keyword domains. In this niche, the domain string is the one SEO lever with no payoff. The levers that do pay are page-level content in the fragmented spicy, truth-or-dare and long-distance clusters, and a brand that people and AI systems can name. That makes the real risk legal, not technical. The decision that matters is whether "Tandem" survives a class 9/41 clearance, because the domain itself costs $11 and the keyword names cost $4,195 for no ranking gain.

One gap is worth closing early. "Couple game" may be the India term, but it shares results with wedding games, so the India landing page should be shaped by Keyword Planner data, not by the US playbook. Until those numbers exist, the safe sequence is: register the brand domain and its redirects, clear the trademark, and ship the three priority-1 pages behind the existing Level 1-3 landing test. Spend nothing on keyword domains unless revenue later justifies buying one as a redirect.
