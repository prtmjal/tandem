# Check Yestwice first; retire Tandem now

The best name on the evidence gathered is **Yestwice**, with **yestwice.com** as the first domain to check, but it is a name to check, not a name confirmed as buyable: **no domain's availability could be verified in this session**. On 14 November 2026 every registry, registrar and WHOIS lookup was blocked by the session's network settings, and a request to allow the registry hosts was declined, so nothing below says any domain is free. What the research does settle is the shape of the answer. Target the **United States first, then the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia**, which share one language and one trusted ending (.com); India stays low priority, as the founder judged. **Drop "Tandem"**: a language-exchange app holds a US registration for the word in software classes, at least four other couples or dating apps already use it, and "After Dark" adds an adult signal that works against a game whose content is suggestive at most. **Do not buy an exact-match domain**: it earns no ranking bonus, cannot be protected as a trademark, and gives AI assistants nothing to cite. Choose a distinctive brand on .com, and if the bare .com is taken, add one plain word ("game") instead of switching to a keyword name. The ranked shortlist is Yestwice, Bothkeys, Kisslock, Lamplit, Bothsaid, Nodtwice, Duskdeck, Twinwick, Entente, Coyly, Twilit and Hushdeck, each screened only by one or two web searches for trademark and app-store conflicts. A ready-to-run check near the end of this report tests all of them in about a minute; its output decides the final pick.

## Four English-speaking markets carry the revenue; India pays about 1% as much per install

The United States is far ahead on every spending measure found. It took **$14.8 billion of the world's $43.6 billion in-app revenue in the second quarter of 2026** ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/global-mobile-iap-revenue-reached-436bn-up-53-year-on-year/)), earns about **$4.60 per app download** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/india-is-starting-to-pay-for-apps-not-just-download-them/)), is the largest dating-app market at over $7 billion across May 2022 to April 2025 ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/state-of-dating-apps-jp)), and generates about 30% of global searches for "couples games" on the one estimate available ([RankHero](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/couples-games)). India sits at the other end: about **$0.03 per download** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/india-app-downloads-revenue-gap-sensor-tower-report-126042400618_1.html)) and outside the top 20 markets for consumer app spending in 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/21/indias-app-downloads-rebounded-to-25-5-billion-in-2025-fueled-by-ai-assistants-and-microdrama-boom/)). That is roughly one-hundredth of the US rate per install.

India also carries the most legal and payment friction of the candidates. In July 2025 the government ordered 25 streaming services blocked for content the press described as "soft porn", not hardcore ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/govt-bans-25-ott-apps-websites-for-streaming-soft-porn-content-486300-2025-07-25)), and Stripe serves Indian businesses by invitation only ([Stripe](https://support.stripe.com/questions/accepting-international-payments-from-stripe-accounts-in-india)). The founder's low-priority call is supported, with one correction on trend: Indian in-app revenue grew 35% year on year to a record $345 million in the second quarter of 2026 ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/india-mobile-app-market-q2-2026)). India is worth leaving open for free reach and revisiting later. It is not worth shaping the name or domain around.

The proposed order is a judgement built on general app-spend, language and legal proxies, because no public source splits revenue by country for Paired, Lovewick, Desire or Coral.

Positions two to four are close and could swap with real per-country data. The Netherlands, Germany, Norway, Denmark and Sweden all score in the "very high" band of the 2025 EF English index ([EF](https://www.ef.com/ca/about-us/press/articles/2025/ef-english-proficiency-index-2025-launched/)), but that index measures self-selected test takers, and a suggestive game depends on nuance, so they are secondary until the game is translated.

Legal friction in the top four is manageable for this product. The rules there target pornography. The UK requires age checks on sites hosting pornography, and Ofcom's largest fine so far is £1.35 million ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/ofcom-fines-porn-company-1.35-million-for-not-having-age-checks)). The Texas model, upheld by the Supreme Court in June 2025, applies only where more than a third of a site's material is sexual material harmful to minors ([Texas statute](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._civ._practice_&_remedies_code_section_129b.002)). Australia's codes use broader wording, "adult or high-impact content" ([eSafety](https://esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/online-safety-codes-introduce-real-world-protections-for-children-online)). No source found addresses suggestive text-only content directly, so the game's "suggestive at most" rule is what keeps it outside these regimes, and a name with no sexual word helps make that case to every reviewer.

One .com serves all four markets. Country endings are preferred by local shoppers in registry-funded surveys, for example three in four Australian consumers say they trust a .au business more ([auDA](https://www.auda.org.au/news-insights/statements/australians-seek-au-online-as-a-marker-of-trust/)), but those surveys ask about buying from a local shop, not opening a game from a shared link. No 2023 to 2026 survey was found showing any new ending is trusted more than .com.

## "Tandem" cannot be kept: one registered owner and at least four same-name couples apps

"Tandem" fails as the lead brand on both law and findability. A US trademark **TANDEM, Reg. 5592755, is listed as owned by Tripod Technology GmbH in Classes 9, 38 and 42** ([Markinton](https://markinton.com/trademark/tandem-86620583)), and the language-exchange app's store listing states the Tandem trademarks belong to that company ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tandem-practice-languages/id959001619)). Its product pairs two people through an app to talk, which is close to a couples conversation game in both function and channel. A second TANDEM word mark, from a medical-device company, covers Classes 9, 10, 41 and 44 ([Markinton](https://markinton.com/trademark/tandem-97566932)), and TANDEM MARRIAGE is listed as registered in Class 45 for a relationship-information website ([Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/866/99/tandemmarriage-86699559.html)). These come from third-party aggregators; current status must be confirmed on the official registers.

The store shelves are just as crowded. "Tandem: For Couples" is a live shared-calendar app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/es/app/id6758865751)), "Tandem - Living Together" serves couples who share a home ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1636705869)), "Tandem: Match on Dates" has partners swipe together on date spots ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/tandem-match-on-dates/6753927747)), and a further private couples app called Tandem is on Product Hunt ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/tandem-10)). There is also a video game, "Tandem: A Tale of Shadows" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1436920/Tandem/)), and playtandem.com was a live children's app when checked on 5 November 2026 ([playtandem.com](https://www.playtandem.com/)). A partner told "it's called Tandem" will land on the wrong product.

Adding "After Dark" does not fix this. Descriptive words added to someone else's registered mark do not normally avoid confusion, and the phrase reads as a late-night edition of the language app, which gives its owner a reason to object. The suffix is not distinctive in games either: a title called "Together After Dark" exists ([Gamebrain](https://gamebrain.co/game/together-after-dark)). "After Dark" also reads as adult to payment and ad reviewers. Stripe does not support businesses selling adult content ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)), and Meta bars ads that discuss sexual practices ([Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/adult-sexual-solicitation-and-sexually-explicit-language/)). How classifiers would score the phrase was not tested; it is a risk taken for no gain.

The risk rating is high for "Tandem" alone and medium-high for "Tandem After Dark". This reverses the 5 November report in this project, which picked tandemafterdark.com on condition that the word cleared; the deeper search shows it does not. Whether tandemafterdark.com is registered is unverified and no longer matters. The game is browser-only, which lowers exposure to app-store disputes today, but a later native app or trademark filing would meet the problem then. Changing before launch is the cheap moment. "After Dark" can stay inside the game as a level or mood label.

## A brand on .com wins; an exact-match domain buys a weak name, not a ranking

The founder is open to an exact-match domain, so the case deserves a fair hearing. Its one measured advantage is clicks from strangers: a Verisign analysis of 11 million search results found results with a query word in the domain were clicked 21.79% of the time against 12.44% without ([Verisign](https://blog.verisign.com/domain-names/how-keyword-rich-domain-names-positively-affect-search-click-through-results/)). That study is from 2015, did not control for ranking position, and was published by the company that runs .com. No newer measurement exists.

Against that sit four structural costs. First, **no ranking benefit**: Google's John Mueller answered "No difference" when asked how keyword domains rank against others ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-no-seo-bonus-for-keyword-based-domains/438324/)), and the 2024 leak of Google's internal documentation shows an attribute named `exactMatchDomainDemotion` ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-search-document-leak-ranking-442617)). Every new site starts slow whatever its name: only 1.74% of new pages reached Google's top 10 within a year in a sample of a million ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank-in-google-and-how-old-are-top-ranking-pages/)). Second, **no site name**: Google's guidance says a generic name is unlikely to be shown as the site name in results ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names?authuser=9)), and Mueller warned in late 2025 that a generic, competitive name can struggle to rank even for itself ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-reasonable-site-name-seo-rank-40660.html)). Third, **no trademark**: a "generic.com" name can be registered in the US only with proof that consumers see it as a brand ([Fish & Richardson](https://www.fr.com/news-insights/articles/supreme-court-finds-booking-com-protectable-rejects-sweeping-rule-denying-protection-to-generic-com-marks/)), which a newcomer cannot show, and the App Store already holds a crowd of near-identical titles such as "Couple Games: 2 Player Games" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6761193720)). Fourth, **no AI visibility**: across 75,000 brands, branded web mentions correlated 0.664 with appearing in AI Overviews, against 0.218 for backlinks ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/?re=true)). "Couples game" in a Reddit thread is a category word; a coined name is a countable mention. And because only 37.9% of AI Overview citations now come from the organic top 10 ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-rankings-ai-citations)), a new, clearly named site can be cited before it ranks.

The market agrees. Across two checks nine days apart and six phrasings, no exact-match domain surfaced for "couples game", "games for couples" or "questions for couples"; the results belong to brands and publishers such as Paired and Plarium ([Paired](https://paired.com/articles/best-online-games-for-couples); [Plarium](https://plarium.com/en/blog/best-games-for-couples-online-in-2026-play-together-anywhere/)). These observations come from a US search tool, not Google itself; UK, Canadian and Australian results could not be observed. The exact-match names with real demand are also the ones already held by resellers: couplesgames.com was listed at $4,195 ([HugeDomains](https://www.hugedomains.com/domain_profile.cfm?d=couplesgames.com)) and couplegame.com showed a for-sale page ([couplegame.com](https://couplegame.com/)) when checked on 5 November 2026. A list of 101 keyword candidates was drawn up for this research and none could be checked. The patterns most likely to be unregistered (twophonegame, couplesnightgame) are the ones with no measured search demand, which removes the reason to want them.

The hybrid is the right fallback, not the first choice. Working couples products use a brand alone (paired.com, flamme.app) or a brand plus one plain word: privegame.com, trycandle.app, connectedcouples.app ([Privé](https://privegame.com/en/blog/spicy-questions-for-couples); [Candle](https://www.trycandle.app/blog/questions-to-ask-your-boyfriend); [Connected Couples](https://www.connectedcouples.app/couples-games-online)). A hybrid keeps a registrable core word and shows a query word in the address. For this product the added word should be **"game" as a suffix**, giving NAMEgame.com and play.NAMEgame.com. A "play" prefix would produce play.playNAME.com. The order of preference is therefore NAME.com, then NAMEgame.com, then getNAME.com, then NAME.app at roughly $14 a year to renew ([domainoffer.net](https://domainoffer.net/tld/app/cloudflare)). Avoid .game, which costs about $309 a year ([domainoffer.net](https://domainoffer.net/tld/game/porkbun)).

The planned layout needs no change. Google treats a subdomain as its own site for site names and Search Console ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names?authuser=9)), so pages meant to rank belong in folders of the main domain. That is where the home page and blog will sit, and the game on play.NAME.com is not meant to rank. Keyword demand is then served by pages (for example /couples-games and /questions-for-couples), not by the domain.

## One minute at a terminal turns this list into an answer

**Recommendation: check in the table's order and take the first name that passes all three gates: the registry check, a normal price at a registrar, and a trademark search.** In practice, check yestwice.com first. If it returns 404 and prices at about $11, hold it and send Yestwice to a trademark lawyer. If it is taken, check bothkeys.com on the same terms before considering yestwicegame.com: a clean bare .com on the second-choice name beats a suffixed domain on the first, because the bare name is what people will type after hearing it once. Go to the "game" suffix only when no bare .com in the top six passes.

The check below is the loop from the research notes with the shortlist filled in. It needs a normal internet connection and reaches two hosts, rdap.verisign.com and pubapi.registry.google. **HTTP 404 means unregistered at the registry. HTTP 200 means taken.** Any other code (for example 429 or 000) means the lookup failed; run that name again. **A 404 is not yet "buyable"**: premium and registry-reserved names also return 404, so every 404 still needs a price check at a registrar. Seven keyword names and tandemafterdark.com are included for information only.

```bash
while read d; do
  case "$d" in
    *.com) u="https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/$d" ;;
    *.net) u="https://rdap.verisign.com/net/v1/domain/$d" ;;
    *.app|*.dev) u="https://pubapi.registry.google/rdap/domain/$d" ;;
    *) u="https://rdap.org/domain/$d" ;;
  esac
  echo "$d $(curl -sL -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' --max-time 20 "$u")"
  sleep 0.5
done < names.txt
```

Send back the output as printed, one domain and one code per line. The remaining steps run in this order.

|---|---|---|

Several things would change the order. If Yestwice and Bothkeys both fail on the bare .com, Kisslock or Lamplit on a bare .com outranks either of them with a suffix. If a 404 turns out to be premium-priced, treat it as taken and move on. If a lawyer judges the "YES!APP +" or BOTH WISE marks too close, the matching name drops out and Nodtwice or Bothsaid stand in, though Bothsaid shares the "Both" element. If translation into German, French or Spanish becomes a near-term plan, Entente and Lamplit rise and Bothkeys, Bothsaid and Hushdeck fall, because their sounds are hard for those speakers. If paid social ads become the main channel, test Kisslock against ad review before committing, because of "kiss". And if the founder still wants an exact-match domain after reading the costs above, the sensible use is a cheap unregistered one held as a redirect to the brand, never as the brand itself.

## Conclusion

The domain is the cheapest part of this decision and the least certain. Every strategic question the founder asked has an answer the evidence supports: four English-speaking markets, no Tandem, no keyword domain, a coined brand on .com with "game" as the fallback suffix. The only open question is the factual one this session could not reach, and it takes about a minute to close. Until the check is run, the shortlist is a queue, not a result.

Two things follow that were not obvious at the start. The name change is worth more than the domain: a product that people can name aloud and AI assistants can cite gains the most from a distinctive word, and the legal exposure of "Tandem" grows with every month of use. And timing favours speed. Interest in couple card games peaks at the end of the year ([Accio](https://www.accio.com/business/couple-card-games-trends)), so a name held and under legal review within the next two weeks can still be live for December and Valentine's Day.
