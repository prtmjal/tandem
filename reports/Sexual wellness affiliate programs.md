# Keep Tandem's shop links local, labelled and quiet

Tandem can add affiliate links safely, but they will earn little, and **every commission rate below must be checked in a network dashboard before launch**, because every research pass in this project was blocked from the networks' and regulators' own pages. The best-documented options are **Amorelie on Awin for Germany, Austria and Switzerland (20%, 45-day attribution)**, **Lovehoney AU on Commission Factory for Australia (22.5%, 30 days)**, **Passage du Desir on Awin for the Netherlands and France (18%, 30 days)**, **Sinful on Awin for the Nordics**, **Lovehoney on Impact or Awin for the UK and US**, and **MyMuse plus Amazon.in for India**. Show links only in an allowlist of countries. Do not use a denylist: sale or import of sex toys is illegal or actively enforced against in the Gulf states, Iran, Pakistan, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Vietnam and the Maldives, and Brazil, Singapore and the Philippines need a legal check first. One disclosure covers India, the US and the UK at once: an **"Ad" label plus a plain sentence saying Tandem earns a commission**, placed on the link block itself. This beats "affiliate", which the UK's ASA advises against and the FTC calls unclear. Build the links as a server `/go` redirect that counts only a daily total, with no referrer, no sub-IDs tied to people or cards, and no prefetching. The phone picks the country from time zone and language and computes "Tonight's kit" from cards both partners actually saw. On realistic traffic, expect **tens to low hundreds of dollars a month**. Treat the links as a service to players and a small top-up to the Couple Pass, not a revenue line.

## Awin, Impact and Commission Factory carry most of the useful programmes

Sexual wellness retailers sit on mainstream networks. Networks treat "adult" as a restricted category, not a banned one: Impact's brand help centre lists "adult content (age-restricted 18+ or NSFW)" next to CBD and vaping as a promotion limit that changes which partners Impact recommends, but it does not bar them ([Impact help](https://help.impact.com/brand/readme/performance-program/recruiting-partners/avoid-recommended-partners-with-promotion-limits)). The real gate is each brand's own approval of the publisher. No source found any network setting a minimum domain age or traffic level, and no source said whether these programmes accept an India-based publisher or a web app instead of a content site. Every brand application should therefore describe the placement honestly: "web app (PWA) plus landing page; an optional, labelled shop shelf on the map screen and a short kit list after a night, never during play".

Awin is the single most valuable account for Europe. Its public merchant profiles give the only figures in this research that come straight from a network rather than an aggregator. **Amorelie Austria offers 20% to publishers other than voucher portals, with a 45-day attribution window and an average basket of about €75** ([Awin 16499](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/16499)). **Passage du Desir's Dutch and German programmes pay 18% with a 30-day cookie** ([Awin 130861](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/130861); [Awin 130859](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/130859)). **Unbound pays 5-15%** ([Awin 95523](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/95523)). Sinful's listings conflict: the French one says 20%, while the UK one says "up to 25%" and "10% standard" ([Awin 24301](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/24301); [Awin 24299](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/24299)). Eis.de, Germany's largest erotic lifestyle shop, is reported at 22-30% with a 30-day cookie on Awin, but only via an aggregator ([affi.io](https://affi.io/m/eis-de)). Awin charges new publishers a refundable 1-unit verification fee, which is returned with the first payment ([Awin](https://success.awin.com/articles/en_US/Knowledge/Why-do-I-have-to-pay-the-sign-up-fee)).

Lovehoney is the one brand that spans the UK, US, Canada, Europe and Australia. That makes it the natural default wherever it runs a programme, but its network has moved over the years, and the sources disagree. LinkClicky places the GB, US and Canada programmes on Impact at roughly 8-19% ([LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/lovehoney/)). An aggregator lists the US programme on Awin with a 120-day cookie ([affi.io](https://affi.io/m/lovehoney)). FlexOffers lists separate UK, US, CA, DE, FR, EU and NZ programmes with 30-day cookies ([FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/lovehoney-affiliate-program/)). Australia is the clearest case: **Lovehoney AU is on Commission Factory at 22.5% of sale value**. The current listing says 30 days, while older material says 90 ([Commission Factory directory](https://commissionfactory.com/advertiser-directory/normal-affiliate-program/89445)). FlexOffers is a sub-network that sits on top of the Awin and Impact programmes, so use it only to fill gaps, because it takes a cut.

Brand-direct programmes from toy makers pay less and vary more. WOW Tech's Womanizer and We-Vibe run on Impact at a reported 1-20% ([LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/we-vibe/)), while a combined worldwide listing shows only 2-4% ([Shopper.com](https://shopper.com/partnerships/we-vibe-com-affiliate-program)). LELO is reported at 15% in the UK, at least 15% in France, and up to 20% depending on publisher type, which suggests an in-app publisher may get a lower tier ([affi.io LELO UK](https://affi.io/m/lelo-uk); [affi.io LELO FR](https://affi.io/m/lelo-fr)). Dame, a good fit for couples, is on Impact at up to 15% with a 30-day cookie ([affpaying](https://www.affpaying.com/dameproducts); [The Affiliate Monkey](https://theaffiliatemonkey.com/affiliate/dame-products-affiliate-program/)). Avoid three options. Babeland bans direct linking and allows only programme-issued codes ([affi.io](https://affi.io/m/babeland)). Bondara's programme has closed ([Paid On Results](https://www.paidonresults.com/merchants/bondara.html)). AliExpress's affiliate programme reportedly bans adult content and carries a 0% "special category" ([Awin AliExpress UK terms](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/7035)).

India works differently. Brands run through Indian cost-per-sale sub-networks (Cuelinks, EarnKaro, vCommission, INRDeals), which take a margin, so a brand's headline rate overstates what Tandem would receive. **MyMuse**, which sells couples' toys, lubricants and massage products, is listed on FlexOffers at **9% with a 30-day cookie** and runs a vCommission programme that accepts app traffic ([FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/mymuse-affiliate-program/); [vCommission](https://www.vcommission.com/blog/publishers/affiliate-marketing-with-mymuse-navigating-a-niche-market-for-affiliates/)). Durex is reported at 15-21.6% on Cuelinks ([Cuelinks](https://www.cuelinks.com/blog/top-health-fitness-affiliate-programs-india/)). Nykaa pays up to 7% to new customers and 3.5% to returning ones, confirming in about 90 days ([EarnKaro](https://earnkaro.com/blog/nykaa-affiliate-program)). Cuelinks also lists Cake, a lubricant and hygiene brand ([Cuelinks](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/cake-affiliate-program)). No public programme was found for IMbesharam, ThatsPersonal or Kaamastra. Skip Bold Care despite its EarnKaro rate of "up to 35%": its line is mostly men's performance supplements, which invites health-claim problems and fits a couples' game poorly.

Amazon belongs only in the generic-item slot. Its policies make a site "unsuitable" if it "promote[s] or contain[s] sexually explicit or obscene materials", and Amazon keeps sole discretion over what counts ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/amazon-associates/amazon-associates-program-policies/provision/CA-P-064458/prohibition-on-sexually-explicit-site-content/)). In 2026, bloggers saw adult book titles suddenly marked "excluded" ([I Heart Sapphfic](https://iheartsapphfic.com/2026/04/06/update-on-amazon-associates-spoiler-its-still-concerning/)). US Health & Personal Care pays about 1% in most guides, and new accounts close unless they make 3 qualifying sales within 180 days ([Lasso](https://getlasso.co/amazon-affiliate-commission-rate/); [ainfluencer](https://ainfluencer.com/amazon-affiliate-requirements/)). India is the exception worth taking: Amazon.in's excluded-products page lists "No excluded ASINs at present" ([Amazon.in](https://affiliate-program.amazon.in/help/operating/exclusions)), Health & Beauty is reported at about 3% ([Cuelinks](https://www.cuelinks.com/blog/amazon-affiliate-commission-rates-guide/)), and Amazon is the obvious Indian shop for candles, blindfolds, silk scarves and feathers.

## An allowlist, not a denylist, decides where links appear

The safest rule is the opposite of how most apps do it. **Show the Toy box only in countries on an explicit allowlist (the rows above that name a primary retailer), and show nothing everywhere else**, including when the phone's country cannot be determined. A denylist fails open: any country nobody researched, such as Egypt, Algeria, Afghanistan or Yemen, would get links by default. An allowlist fails closed. This matches the product's rule that the safe outcome is the default.

The countries that must get no links fall into three groups. The first group has strong evidence of illegality or active enforcement. In **Malaysia**, sale is prohibited under the Printing Presses and Publications Act, and customs seized 1,240 toys at Port Klang in August 2026 and an RM1m stash in January 2026 ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2024/02/29/previously-used-against-watches-and-now-sex-toys-lawyers-explain-the-breadth-of-malaysias-printing-laws/120369); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/08/14/over-1200-sex-toys-seized-by-customs-dept-at-port-klang)). In **Thailand**, Criminal Code Section 287 treats toys as obscene objects, punishable by up to three years in prison, and a 2024 reform bill failed at first reading ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/thailand/40026922); [Bangkok Post](https://bangkokpost.com/thailand/politics/2833146/adult-biz-reform-in-the-works)). In **Pakistan**, buying, selling or advertising toys is punishable under obscenity law, and there was an arrest in Lahore in 2017 ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/pakistan-sex-toy-black-market-dildos-butt-plugs)). The **UAE** confiscates toys and fines people over them, **Saudi Arabia** prohibits import of "sexual ... tools and devices", and **Bahrain** destroys flagged shipments ([Goods Across Borders: UAE](https://www.goodsacrossborders.com/traveling/can-i-bring/sex-toys/to/uae/print); [FedEx MEISA](https://www.fedex.com/content/dam/fedex/meisa-middle-east/downloads/fedex-meisa-restricted-items.pdf); [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/toronto/article/stop-sending-butt-plugs-to-bahrain-toronto-sex-store-receives-letters-from-us-department-of-war/)). **Indonesia** has burned seized toys, **Vietnam** blocks imports at customs, and the **Maldives** prohibits them as pornographic material ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/wx8j9x/indonesia-burned-hundreds-of-sex-toys-amid-spiking-demand); [Pretoria News](https://pretorianews.co.za/saturday-star/news/2025-01-24-when-pleasure-meets-the-law-countries-with-sex-toy-bans)).

The second group rests on weaker but consistent evidence: **Qatar, Kuwait, Oman and Iran**, documented only through traveller and shipping guides ([Doha Guides](https://www.dohaguides.com/qatar-customs-prohibited-items/comment-page-1/); [Pretoria News](https://pretorianews.co.za/saturday-star/news/2025-01-24-when-pleasure-meets-the-law-countries-with-sex-toy-bans)). **Bangladesh and Brunei** are excluded as a precaution, with no source found. In none of these countries do neutral items such as candles justify the risk of appearing under a "sexual wellness" heading.

The third group is legal to sell in but held back for now. **Brazil** is a legal market with a couples-focused shop on Lomadee, but its ECA Digital statute, in force since 17 March 2026, bans self-declaration of age for content "inappropriate" for under-18s ([Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/brazil-digital-eca-brazils-child-and-adolescent-statute-a-new-framework-for-online-protection-of-children-and-adolescents-2/)). A shelf of sexual wellness products is a much stronger candidate for "inappropriate" than the game's text, and Tandem relies on an 18+ tap. Hold Brazil until counsel answers that question. **Singapore and the Philippines** stay off because their legal status was not confirmed, and the regional marketplaces are closed to this category anyway: Shopee's affiliate rules prohibit promoting sex toys and pay no commission on them, and Lazada PH bars sexual promotional content ([Shopee MY](https://help.shopee.com.my/portal/10/article/140075-[ENG]-Shopee-Affiliates-Social-Media-Community-Guidelines); [Shopee SG](https://help.shopee.sg/portal/10/article/132082-FAQs%20on%20Prohibited%20Items); [Lazada PH](https://www.lazada.com.ph/wow/i/ph/corp/affiliate-terms)).

India itself stays on the allowlist. No statute bans or permits sex toys, and the risk comes from obscenity law (IPC 292, now BNS 294) and a 1964 customs notification ([Nyaaya](https://nyaaya.org/nyaaya-weekly/what-is-the-law-on-sale-of-sex-toys-in-india/)). Both the Bombay High Court (March 2024) and the Delhi High Court (judgment reported November 2025) rejected treating "body massagers" as obscene because of an imagined use. Text links to mainstream retailers, with neutral names, no explicit images and behind an 18+ gate, sit far from IT Act s.67/67A territory, but that is an inference, not a ruling.

### Choosing the country on the phone

Do the detection on the phone and never send it anywhere as data. Use `Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone` mapped to a country through a small static table built from the IANA zone list, and use the region subtags of `navigator.languages` (for example, `en-GB` gives GB) as a second signal. Apply three rules in order. First, if either signal points to a no-links country, show nothing; when signals conflict, the stricter one wins. Second, if the time zone maps to an allowlisted country, use it. Third, if the time zone is ambiguous (`UTC`, `Etc/*`), use the language region only if it is allowlisted. Otherwise, show nothing. A small "Shopping from: United Kingdom · Change" picker inside the Toy box handles expats and travellers. It lists only allowlisted countries and is hidden entirely when the time zone is in a no-links country. Store the choice in `localStorage` as a per-phone convenience, wrapped in try/catch. Each partner's phone decides on its own, and nothing about one partner's shop setting or taps ever reaches the other phone.

## One label satisfies India, the US and the UK together

The three regimes agree more than they differ. The words each one accepts point to the same answer. **The UK ASA/CMA guide recommends "Ad", "Advert", "Advertising" or "Advertisement", and advises against "Affiliate" and abbreviations** ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html)). The ASA has ruled the tag "aff" insufficient, and it holds the brand and the affiliate jointly responsible ([RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/winter-2024/influencers-and-affiliate-links-the-whole-marketing-chain-must-know-the-rules/)). The FTC's 2023 Endorsement Guides require a disclosure that is "difficult to miss (i.e., easily noticeable) and easily understandable by ordinary consumers" ([Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/an-in-depth-look-at-the-ftcs-updates-2)). Commentary on the FTC's FAQ says "affiliate link" alone is not clear enough, while "paid link" next to the link can work, and that footer or behind-a-"more" placements fail ([PartnerCentric](https://partnercentric.com/blog/how-to-properly-disclose-ftc-endorsements/)). India's ASCI accepts "Ad", "Advertisement", "Sponsored", "Collaboration" and "Partnership", and rejects disclosures buried among links ([Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/ASCI-releases-influencer-advertising-guidelines-for-digital-media-Effective-14-June-21-onward)). The Department of Consumer Affairs' 2023 endorsement guidance under the CCPA framework adds that **the disclosure must be prominent, separate from platform tools, and in the same language as the endorsement** ([DoCA](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/sites/default/files/PIB1892527.pdf)). In the EU, commentators read an undisclosed affiliate link as a potentially misleading commercial practice under the UCPD ([Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/4/influencer-marketing-practices-under-scrutiny-in-europe)). The Digital Fairness Act is still only a proposal ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/aut/legal-updates/inside-the-eu-s-planned-digital-fairness-act-digital-design-subscription-traps-and-influencer-marketing)).

"Ad" is the only word that all three accept, so it leads. A plain sentence then does the FTC's "easily understandable" work without leaning on the word "affiliate". Use this exact wording, in neutral ink, on the heading of both the Tonight's kit block and the Toy box, visible before any tap:

> **Ad · Paid links**
> If you buy through these links, Tandem earns a commission. Your price stays the same. We picked these ourselves, and the shop never learns what you played.

Put this line under each product tile, in the same visual weight as the product name:

> Ad · Opens [Shop name] in your browser

On Amazon tiles, add the line Amazon requires. This wording is widely known but was not verified in this session:

> As an Amazon Associate, Tandem earns from qualifying purchases.

Show this one-line notice on the first tap per session, before leaving the app:

> You're leaving Tandem for [Shop name]. Their cookies and privacy policy apply from here.

Add a sentence to `/privacy` covering the same facts. If the interface is ever localised, translate all of this into the interface language, as the Indian guidance requires (for example, Hindi: "विज्ञापन · पेड लिंक"). "Your price stays the same" must stay true, so drop it for any retailer that prices affiliate traffic differently. Never invent star ratings, "couples love this" quotes or "bestseller" badges. The FTC's rule on consumer reviews, in force since 21 October 2024, carries civil penalties for fake testimonials ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). Make no health or performance claims, which would also bring in India's misleading-advertisement rules.

## Build the links so the server learns a count and nothing else

The link architecture should leave nothing behind that a regulator, a network or a curious partner could read. A tap on a sexual wellness link can reveal interest in someone's sex life, which is special-category data under GDPR Article 9. Tandem's design goal is that **the shop learns only that "someone arrived", and Tandem learns only "one more tap today"**.

Build the redirect as a server route, `GET /go/:country/:item`. It looks up a static, versioned map in a new shared module (for example `shared/shop.ts`) holding each network's deep link. It returns a `302` only for keys in that map, so the route can never act as an open redirect. It increments a single daily total in `server/stats.ts`, and that is all it records. Add one new event such as `shopTaps: 'Shop link taps (totals)'`, split by surface (kit or Toy box) at most. Never split it by item, country or card. A tally of "blindfold" taps is a tally of which cards people played, and rule 10 forbids anything about cards. Log no IP, user agent, room code or path, and send `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, `Cache-Control: no-store` and `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` on the redirect.

The anchor itself should be `<a href="/go/gb/blindfold" target="_blank" rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer" referrerpolicy="no-referrer">`. Google asks for `rel="sponsored"` on affiliate links ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2021/07/link-tagging-and-link-spam-update)). `target="_blank"` hands the link to the system browser, which preserves attribution better than a webview. Never append per-person or per-room sub-IDs. At most, use a static tag per surface (`kit`, `toybox`) and only if a programme asks for one. Some programmes validate the referring domain. For those, relax the policy to `strict-origin`, which sends the domain only, never the path. Exclude `/go/` from service-worker precaching, add `Disallow: /go/` to `robots.txt`, and never prefetch or preload these URLs. A cookie set without a real tap is cookie stuffing, which leads to reversed commissions and termination ([Involve Asia](https://involve.asia/?p=69981)). Some individual programmes ban masked redirects, though Awin and CJ have no network-wide ban ([MakeInfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/affiliate-link-cloaking-why-brands-restrict-it-and-what-disclosure-rules-still-apply)). The visible "Opens [Shop name]" label keeps the destination honest, and any programme that forbids redirects gets a direct `href` instead. Amazon in particular bars link shortening that hides the destination ([QualityUnit](https://cdn.qualityunit.com/faq/should-you-cloak-affiliate-links)).

The ePrivacy cookie rule attaches to whoever stores or reads data on the device ([CookieYes](https://www.cookieyes.com/blog/affiliate-cookie-consent-requirements/)). Tandem sets nothing, and the network sets its cookie on its own domain after the tap, so Tandem needs no cookie banner for this. One open question remains: Awin asks publishers to pass TCF consent parameters in the link ([Awin wiki](https://wiki.awin.com/index.php/Consent_Signals)), while Webgains puts the duty on merchants ([Webgains](https://www.webgains.com/public/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/16380)). Ask Awin in writing what a publisher without a consent tool should send. Do not add a consent manager to the app.

Several product rules keep the links from becoming pressure. Tonight's kit appears only on the end-of-night screen, after the night is finished and after the private share opt-in step. It never appears during a night and never on the share card. It starts collapsed as one row ("Tonight's kit · Ad") and lists at most three items. It is computed on the phone from cards that were actually shown on both phones and completed with Next: never from a passed card, never from a card swapped after a private vote, never from a face-down mystery card, and never from any vote, ceiling or "curious" tap. Gear for a card someone passed on would be a nudge. Gear for a card that never surfaced would leak that a partner voted yes. If the night matched nothing, the kit simply does not appear; there is no generic filler. Apart-mode nights rarely include hands-on cards, so the kit will usually be absent there, which is correct. The Toy box lives on the map next to the Couple Pass offer. A device-level setting, "Show shop links", lets either partner turn all of it off on their own phone. **No link, tap or purchase may ever unlock cards, discount the Couple Pass or change progress**, because that would make the traffic "incentivized" and break rule 7. Add these as rule 12 in `CLAUDE.md`, with a test that `Room.view(me)` exposes nothing new.

## The founder signs up in a fixed order, starting with the domain

Approval depends on what a brand's reviewer sees, so the domain and a finished public site come first. That means a landing page that is safe for work, `/privacy`, an about-and-contact page with an address on the domain, a short "How Tandem makes money" page, the 18+ gate, and a screenshot of the Toy box showing where links will sit. Applying from a bare `onrender.com` subdomain or with no visible product invites rejection. Then set up banking and tax, because networks ask for tax forms and payout details at sign-up.

## Illustrative earnings stay small until nights reach the tens of thousands

The figures below are **illustrative only**: the assumptions are the author's, and only the ranges come from sources. Published benchmarks are thin. Grips puts sexual wellness conversion at **1.5-2.0%** with category AOV up to about $100 ([Grips](https://gripsintelligence.com/insights/industries/health-beauty/condoms-and-sexual-wellness)). PinkCherry's AOV sits at $50-75 ([Grips](https://d8q56x4xdbci2.cloudfront.net/insights/retailers/pinkcherry.com)), and Amorelie reports a €75 basket ([Awin](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/16499)). The only published EPC found is B-Vibe's $0.03-0.07 ([FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/b-vibe-affiliate-program/)). Attribution loss pulls everything down further. Safari limits script-set cookies to 7 days ([Awin](https://www.awin.com/us/news-and-events/industry-news/cookie-periods-shorten-even-further-with-additional-changes-to-safaris-itp-update)), and couples on phones in a private moment are likely to buy later, if at all.

| Scenario (illustrative) | Nights finished per month | Shop taps (assumed 7.5% of nights) | Conversion | AOV | Net commission | Gross per month | After halving for returns and lost attribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early (mixed traffic) | 2,000 | 150 | 1.5% | $60 | 10% | $13.50 | **about $7** |
| Growing (mostly UK/EU/AU) | 20,000 | 1,500 | 2.0% | $70 | 12% | $252 | **about $125** |
| Established (mostly UK/EU/AU) | 100,000 | 7,500 | 2.0% | $80 | 15% | $1,800 | **about $900** |

The pattern matters more than the numbers. Revenue tracks Western European and Australian traffic, where 18-22% programmes meet €75 baskets. Indian traffic earns about a tenth as much per tap, because Amazon.in's roughly 3% and MyMuse's 9% apply to much smaller orders. At the early stage, payout thresholds mean money arrives every few months, not every month. A handful of Couple Pass sales will out-earn the shop for a long time, which is a reason to keep the shop quiet rather than push it.

This list is the to-do list for the founder's first week inside the dashboards:

- **Rates and terms.** Every commission, cookie and threshold above is unconfirmed on the retailer's own terms. Most comes from aggregators that are often undated and that conflict (Lovehoney: Impact, Awin or Webgains; 30, 90 or 120 days).
- **Publisher eligibility.** Whether these programmes accept an India-based publisher, or a web app as opposed to a content site, is unknown. LELO's rate tiers by publisher type suggest in-app traffic may be paid less.
- **Amazon.** The verbatim "sexually explicit" clause, whether sexual wellness products earn fees at all, whether in-app links are allowed, and the exact disclosure sentence were not read.
- **Awin consent.** Whether Awin's TCF consent parameters are mandatory for a publisher with no consent tool is unknown, and so is whether India-based publishers face extra checks.

- **Legal status.** Singapore and the Philippines were not confirmed. Bangladesh and Brunei have no source. The Gulf states rest on secondary guides, not statutes. Germany's JMStV, Brazil's ECA Digital as applied to product links, and the DSA's reach to first-party affiliate links all need counsel.

- **Network details.** Whether ShareASale still exists separately from Awin is unconfirmed, and so is the Safari change of September 2025.

## Conclusion

The research changes the frame from "which programme pays most" to "where can a link exist at all". Rates cluster at 15-22% for the good European and Australian specialists, so retailer choice barely moves revenue. What moves risk is country routing, and an allowlist that fails closed is cheaper and safer than any lookup table of bans. The same logic applies to privacy. The strongest design choice is to compute the kit on the phone from cards both partners already saw, and let the server learn only a daily count. That keeps the shop inside the game's existing promise that nothing private leaves the room.

Commercially, the shop's value is not its revenue. It is a small, honest service that makes a candle-and-oil card easier to say yes to next time. That argues for building it after the Couple Pass checkout is stable, enabling Europe and Australia first, where the euros are, and India second, where the founder can test it personally, and treating any month above $100 as a sign that the game is growing, not that the shop is working.
