# Card UI, Prompt Formats, Readability and Typography for a Two-Phone Couples Intimacy Game

Research note: several primary hosts (frontiersin.org, fontsinuse.com, mechanicsofmagic.com) were unreachable from this session's network sandbox, so some findings rely on search-result summaries of those pages rather than full-text reads. Those findings are flagged where relevant.

## 1. Why physical conversation-card games feel like a ritual, and what digital adaptations keep or lose

### Takeaway
Physical decks build ritual from a fixed sequence of levels (WNRS: Perception, Connection, Reflection), short pre-written prompts that take the burden of asking off the players, wildcards, a closing card, and flexible "house rules." Reviewers say digital versions that copy the card look keep the conversation intact. What they lose is the tactile, giftable object and the sense of occasion.

### Cited Findings
- WNRS is built around three ordered levels, Perception, Connection and Reflection. — [WNRS official site](https://werenotreallystrangers.com/pages/core-landing-page)
- The core deck is commonly listed at 150 cards. It includes wildcards (action or "do this" cards) and a final closing card. — [Pitt News](https://pittnews.com/article/167282/blogs/offbeat-were-not-really-strangers-the-card-game-that-pushes-me-to-create-meaningful-connections); [Paper Source listing](https://www.papersource.com/gifts/were-not-really-strangers-card-game-0810060650004.html)
- Critical-play analyses (Stanford-style "Mechanics of Magic" student essays, 2024) say the layered levels and divided prompts make the process "feel seamless." They also say pre-written questions let people reach deeper answers "without putting the responsibility or the intrusiveness onto the players." (Read from a search summary; the full text could not be fetched.) — [Mechanics of Magic, Kongmeng 2024](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2024/04/22/critical-play-judging-and-getting-vulnerable-were-not-really-strangers-kongmeng/); [Mechanics of Magic 2024](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2024/04/23/critical-play-were-not-really-strangers-9/)
- Founder Koreen Odiney described WNRS as "a passport to people" (Vogue interview, quoted secondhand). — [Pitt News](https://pittnews.com/article/167282/blogs/offbeat-were-not-really-strangers-the-card-game-that-pushes-me-to-create-meaningful-connections)
- Esther Perel's *Where Should We Begin: A Game of Stories* uses two card types, Prompt cards and Story cards. It has a "simple" rule set (Story cards only, for a first play or dinner table) and an "advanced" rule set with prompts and tokens. The official guidance tells players to "tailor the rules and cards" to the situation. — [estherperel.com How to Play](https://estherperel.com/how-to-play-where-should-we-begin)
- The booklet links by QR code to videos in which Perel explains the simple and advanced versions, so rules are onboarded by a person's voice rather than by dense text. — [Boston Globe, 2021](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2021/12/01/lifestyle/therapist-podcast-star-esther-perel-has-made-card-game-help-players-get-closer)
- Perel: "Each card combination guides us towards mischief and mystery, humor and honesty." The 2nd edition has 200 new prompts and a compact design meant to be "just as mobile as we are now." — [PR Newswire launch](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/esther-perel-launches-new-card-game-where-should-we-begin---a-game-of-stories-301327126.html); [Esther Perel game shop, 2nd ed.](https://game.estherperel.com/products/where-should-we-begin-a-game-of-stories-2nd-edition)
- {THE AND} by The Skin Deep began as an interactive documentary and now ships editions in different colorways (for example Black, Orange and White for the Friends edition). One buyer said "the black ones are tense" and mixes in a lighter deck. This is anecdotal evidence that card colour signals emotional intensity to players. — [The Skin Deep About](https://explore.theskindeep.com/about/); [desertcart listing / reviews](https://www.desertcart.sn/products/486846471-the-and-couples-edition)
- A critical-play essay praised The Skin Deep's digital *Blind Spots* deck for its "black interface, swiping signifiers… elegant typography, and gentle yet profound questions," which made play feel "gentle, relaxed, and calming." This is a rare positive account of a digital card deck. (Search summary.) — [Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=16961)
- On digital versus physical WNRS: one reviewer said the digital version "mimicked the exact look and feel of the physical card game" and gameplay was barely affected. Others say "the physical cards feel different," praise physical quality, and treat apps or PDFs as an "in a pinch" fallback. — [Thingtesting reviews](https://thingtesting.com/brands/were-not-really-strangers/reviews?p=6); [Bark review](https://www.bark.us/content-review/were-not-really-strangers-review/)

### Inferences
- Ritual comes from structure: ordered levels, one prompt at a time, a beginning (rules or intention card) and an end (closing card). It does not come from physicality alone. All of that structure carries over to digital.
- Physical cards also give handling, shuffling and drawing, and a shared object that both people look at. Two phones break the single shared object, so a synced reveal (both cards flip together on a countdown) is the best digital substitute for "drawing a card together."
- Colour-coded levels are a known convention in this genre. Mapping one consistent hue to each intensity level (conversation, closer, suggestive) gives players a pre-verbal cue before they read anything.
- A "simple mode" first session, as in Perel's game, lowers the stakes. A short spoken or visual rules or consent card at the start mirrors the physical rules card.

### Gaps
- No primary interview with Koreen Odiney about how the card visual design (red cards, typography) was chosen was found. The deck's red colour and type choices are known informally, but no citable source turned up in this session.
- No source was found for the Intimacy Deck or BestSelf card design rationale.

## 2. Card UI vs chat/text vs audio; phubbing and when the phone should step back

### Takeaway
Partner phubbing is reliably linked to lower relationship satisfaction and intimacy, so the app should keep the phone's role brief: show the prompt, then get out of the way. The classic "mere presence of a phone hurts conversation" finding has failed to replicate. Audio (Dipsea-style) earns its place through imagination and eyes-free use, but it suits solo or guided listening better than turn-taking conversation.

### Cited Findings
- A 2025 meta-analysis (Frontiers in Psychology) pooled 52 studies, 58 samples and 19,698 participants. It found that partner phubbing negatively relates to relationship satisfaction, marital satisfaction, relationship quality, intimacy, responsiveness and emotional closeness. Media addiction was the strongest antecedent (rZ = 0.492). (From a search summary; full text blocked.) — [Frontiers / PMC meta-analysis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12106345/)
- A 2023 cross-sectional study (n = 308, Turkey) found that more partner phubbing was associated with lower perceived relationship quality and satisfaction. The authors say the direction of the effect is unclear. — [PsyPost, 2023](https://www.psypost.org/2023/02/people-exposed-to-phubbing-by-their-romantic-partner-are-less-satisfied-with-their-romantic-relationship-67708)
- Przybylski and Weinstein's "mere presence of a phone lowers closeness" effect failed to replicate in Linares and Sellier (2021, PLoS ONE, 2 experiments, 136 groups) and in Crowley et al. (2018). The authors cite small original samples and changing phone norms. — [PLoS ONE replication (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8189469/); [FORRT Replication Atlas](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1177/0265407512453827/)
- Audio erotica's claimed advantage is that the listener "can fill in the details" and use imagination. A researcher quoted in the same piece concedes that people responded more to audio-visual than audio-only erotica "in every study." — [Marie Claire on Dipsea](https://marieclaire.com/sex-love/a25449637/audio-porn-app-dipsea); [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/dipsea-quinn-audio-porn-rise-uk/)
- Quinn founder Caroline Spiegel said audio "evokes a sense of mystery and sensuality that video doesn't." Almost half of Quinn's beta users were men, which contradicted the team's assumption. — [Yahoo Finance](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/younger-spiegel-launches-audio-erotica-100029117.html)
- Dipsea's founders (Gina Gutierrez, Faye Keegan) built stories for women who felt erotica "didn't really connect with their lives." — [mindbodygreen](https://www.mindbodygreen.com/articles/dipsea-audio-porn-app-made-for-women)

### Inferences
- The harm in phubbing comes from attention being diverted from the partner. A prompt card that both partners read at the same moment, and then put down, is joint use rather than phubbing. The design goal is the shortest possible "eyes on screen" time per card: read, look up, talk.
- Rules to consider: no feeds, notifications or chat threads inside a round. Dim the card or show an "eyes up" state after the reveal. Do not require a tap to keep the session alive. An optional "lay the phone face down" cue would support the conversation (an inference, not tested).
- A chat or text UI pushes partners to type to each other while sitting together, which works against eye contact. Cards are better suited to co-located play. Text input fits only remote or asynchronous modes.
- Audio is a strong option for the suggestive levels, or for eyes-closed moments, because it frees the eyes entirely. It works against turn-taking conversation and is awkward with two phones playing out of sync. Treat it as optional per card, not as the default format.

### Gaps
- No controlled study comparing card, chat and audio formats for couples' conversation prompts was found. Format recommendations here are expert inference.
- No research was found specifically on shared or joint phone use during couples' activities, as opposed to phubbing.

## 3. Readability: prompt length, font size, line length, dark-mode contrast, typeface legibility, dyslexia

### Takeaway
Prompts should be short (one sentence; see inferences for word counts) and set large. Phones are held at about 30–40 cm, often in dim light, and prompts are read aloud. Keep about 50–75 characters per line or fewer. Use off-white on dark grey rather than pure white on pure black. Make spacing generous, because evidence on dyslexia favours spacing over any particular "dyslexia font."

### Cited Findings
- Baymard recommends 50–75 characters per line. In portrait mobile, line length is rarely too long. Problems appear in landscape past about 80 characters. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability)
- The 45–75 character range traces to Bringhurst. Mobile-specific ranges in secondary sources vary (30–50, 45–65) and none cites a primary study. — [UXPin](https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/optimal-line-length-for-readability/)
- Recommended mobile body size in secondary sources is 16–18px. Others allow 14–16px. No primary research was found for a hard 16px minimum. — [UXPin](https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/optimal-line-length-for-readability/); [Digital Thrive](https://www.digitalthriveai.com/en-ca/resources/web-design/balancing-line-length-font-size-responsive-web-design/)
- Dyslexia guidance citing the BDA recommends a plain, evenly spaced sans serif, 12–14pt or larger (print), with some readers needing larger text. — [Wigan Council Dyslexia Style Guide (citing BDA)](https://www.wigan.gov.uk/Docs/PDF/Business/Professionals/SEN/HEFA/Dyslexia-Style-Guide.pdf)
- Dyslexia Scotland recommends about 1.5× line spacing and states that "recent evidence shows no consistent advantage of sans serif over serif fonts – spacing is a stronger factor than letter form." — [Dyslexia Scotland](https://dyslexiascotland.org.uk/dyslexia-friendly-typed-formats)
- Other dyslexia-friendly guides (Ako Aotearoa 2023, Smashing Magazine) recommend left-aligned text, avoiding justified text, italics and all-caps blocks, and offering a reading mode. — [Ako Aotearoa Dyslexia Friendly Style Guide 2023](https://ako.ac.nz/assets/Knowledge-centre/ALNACC-Resources/Dyslexia-resources/230907-Dyslexia-Friendly-Style-Guide.pdf); [Smashing Magazine](https://uxdesign.smashingmagazine.com/2021/11/dyslexia-friendly-mode-website)
- Dark mode: pure white on pure black (21:1) can cause halation, a glow at letter edges, especially for people with astigmatism. The common fix is a dark grey background (for example Material's #121212 or #1A1A1A) with off-white text (#E0E0E0 to #D4D4D4). The evidence is mostly practitioner guidance rather than controlled studies, and prevalence figures (around 30–33% with astigmatism) vary by source. — [A11y with Diana](https://a11ywithdiana.substack.com/p/and); [Trimble Modus dark mode guidance](https://modus.trimble.com/foundations/dark-mode)
- Bricolage Grotesque (Atelier Triay, OFL) is a variable font with weight, width and optical-size axes. Google describes the opsz axis this way: low values give a body-text design, high values a display design with shorter x-height and more stroke contrast. CSS `font-optical-sizing: auto` is the default in modern browsers. — [Google Fonts glossary: optical size axis](https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/glossary/optical_size_axis); [ateliertriay/bricolage repo listing](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fateliertriay%2Fbricolage)
- Figtree (Erik Kennedy, 2022, OFL on Google Fonts) is a friendly geometric sans with a large x-height, generous apertures and uniform strokes, aimed at UI and body text. — [Google Fonts Figtree](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Figtree/about); [Sessions College](https://sessions.edu/notes-on-design/free-font-friday-figtree); [Pimp my Type](https://pimpmytype.com/font/figtree)

### Inferences
- Prompt length (inference from line-length and font-size constraints): at 24–28px on a 360–390pt-wide screen with 24px side padding, a line holds about 20–28 characters. Fewer than about 25 words (roughly 3–5 lines) keeps the prompt on one screen with no scrolling, readable in one glance and speakable in one breath. Aim for 8–20 words. Put extra instructions in a smaller secondary line.
- Font size for prompts: set the prompt as display text, about 24–32px (clamp to the viewport). Never go below about 20px for prompt text, and keep 16px or more for secondary text. Respect the user's text-size settings (rem units) and test at 200% zoom.
- Bricolage at large prompt sizes is fine, and its display optical size suits headlines. Its quirky ink-trap and grotesque details become less legible at small sizes and in dim light. Use Bricolage for short prompts at 24px and up, and Figtree for everything small (buttons, rules, captions). Consider setting prompt body text in Figtree with Bricolage only for level titles if testing shows long prompts strain. Lock or limit the `wdth` axis so condensed widths are not used for prompts.
- Dark-mode card palette: background around #121212 to #1A1A1A or a deep tinted hue per level. Text around #E8E4DF, warm off-white. Check WCAG AA, 4.5:1 for body and 3:1 for 24px and larger, against any generated art behind the text by adding a scrim.
- Dyslexia: left-align (or centre only short prompts), line-height 1.3–1.5 for display and 1.5 for body, no italics or all-caps for prompts, slight positive letter-spacing on small text, and an optional "larger text / plain background" toggle.

### Gaps
- No primary research on reading aloud at arm's length on phones, or on dim-light reading on mobile, was found.
- The exact Bricolage opsz range and any published legibility testing for Bricolage or Figtree were not found.
- No citable study gives an optimal word count for conversation prompts. The 8–25 word guidance is inference.

## 4. Interaction: tap vs swipe, flip/reveal animation, reduced motion, thumb zones, target sizes

### Takeaway
Use taps on two clearly labelled, equally sized buttons, Pass and Next, at 48px or more, rather than swipe. Swipe is invisible, has Tinder "judge/reject a person" connotations, and makes it easy to skip by accident. Keep the card flip but make it short and honour `prefers-reduced-motion` with an instant swap or crossfade, in both CSS and JS-driven animation.

### Cited Findings
- WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum, AA) requires targets of at least 24×24 CSS px, or 24px spacing. SC 2.5.5 (AAA) requires 44×44. Apple HIG recommends 44pt, and Material recommends 48dp. — [W3C-mirroring summary, WCAG 2.2](https://www.disabilityworld.org/toolkit/standards/wcag/2-5-8-target-size-minimum/); [AllAccessible guide](https://allaccessible.org/blog/wcag-258-target-size-minimum-implementation-guide)
- Gestures are often invisible, so users may never discover them. Visual signifiers increase perceived input options and lower perceived mental effort (Univ. Lille doctoral thesis, 2023). — [Univ. Lille thesis](https://pepite-depot.univ-lille.fr/LIBRE/EDMADIS/2023/2023ULILB045.pdf); [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/in-app-gestures-and-mobile-app-usability-d2e737bd5250)
- Tinder taught the world what "swipe right" means. Commentators argue that the pattern works only where swiping is the app's core concept, and that repurposing it elsewhere can fail. — [ICS](https://www.ics.com/blog/tap-tinder-and-touchscreens-how-swipe-can-improve-user-experience); [Codebridge](https://codebridge.tech/articles/the-impact-of-gestures-on-mobile-user-experience)
- Accessible swipe actions need non-gesture alternatives, such as buttons or menus. — [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/accessible-swipe-contextual-action-triggers)
- Reduced motion: under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, disable 3D card flips (set transition to none, or swap faces instantly) or replace them with opacity fades. JS-driven animations must also be suppressed, and the setting should be watched for runtime changes. Flips must be triggerable by keyboard (Enter/Space) and by tap, not hover-only. Large elements flipping in 3D can cause dizziness or nausea. — [ezud: Animation and Motion Accessibility](https://www.ezud.com/animation-motion-accessibility-reduced-motion); [Quackit 3D card flip](https://www.quackit.com/css/animations/examples/css_animation_3d_card_flip.cfm); [Calgary Summit design system: Rotating Cards](https://summit.calgary.ca/components/marketing-and-storytelling/rotating-cards.html)

### Inferences
- Swipe-left/right on a card of intimate prompts reads as "rejecting" content, or by association a partner, and invites accidental skips. Taps on labelled buttons make the choice deliberate and legible to the other partner.
- Pass and Next with equal weight: same size, same shape, same visual weight (for example both outlined, or both filled in neutral tones), side by side, each at least 48×48px (ideally 56px tall, full-width halves) with 8px or more gap. Pass should never be a smaller text link, a destructive red, or hidden behind a gesture. Show no shame copy, and do not tell the partner why someone passed.
- Thumb zone: place Pass/Next in the bottom third. Keep the prompt in the mid-upper area, which is in view rather than reachable. The prompt needs no interaction.
- Flip and reveal timing (inference from common motion practice): flip about 400–600ms with ease-out, a 3-2-1 countdown at about 1s per step, and an optional subtle haptic on reveal. Under reduced motion, use a 150–200ms crossfade and a numeric countdown with no scaling or pulsing. Keep the 3D rotation on the card only, never the whole viewport.
- Synced reveal: show a "waiting for partner" state on the card back. Both phones flip on a server timestamp so neither partner reads first.

### Gaps
- No primary NN/g article on swipe discoverability was retrieved. The 30–50% "missed swipe" figure seen in a vendor blog was unsourced and is excluded.
- No research was found on the emotional connotation of swipe in non-dating contexts. The Tinder association is expert inference.

## 5. Typography and mood: serif vs sans, variable fonts, letter spacing, handwriting; what Dipsea, Ferly, Hinge and WNRS use

### Takeaway
The evidence is thin. Typeface does shift perceived sincerity and trust, and serifs evoke more emotion-laden adjectives. Category leaders split between warm sans (Hinge) and editorial or serif-led looks (common in sexual-wellness branding, but not verified here). Keeping Bricolage + Figtree is defensible. Add warmth through weight, size, spacing and colour rather than by switching to script.

### Cited Findings
- A Monotype/Neurons study (2022) found that typefaces alone conveyed trust and sincerity. A humanist sans raised perceived sincerity and honesty, and the study found up to a 13% lift in positive consumer response from typeface choice. The press release is the only source, and methodology was unverified. — [Monotype press release](https://cms-prod.monotype.com/company/news/monotype-study-shows-typeface-choice-can-boost-positive-consumer-response-13)
- Serif typefaces elicit more emotion-laden adjectives than sans serifs. Sans serifs still carry distinct personalities. (Frontiers in Psychology 2017, citing Tantillo et al. 1995.) — [Frontiers 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01229/pdf)
- Hinge's rebrand was by Red Antler. Secondary sources describe a plain, friendly sans register and a custom wordmark, with an ink/plum/pink palette. No reliable source says Hinge uses a serif. — [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/hinge-brand-guidelines/); [Hinge brand resources](https://hinge.co/brand-resources)
- Dipsea has a Fonts In Use entry. Its identity "plays off of the layered sound design." The specific typefaces could not be retrieved because the site was blocked. — [Fonts In Use: Dipsea](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/29277/dipsea)

### Inferences
- Bricolage Grotesque has a quirky, warm, slightly handmade feel, especially at heavier weights and wider widths. That suits a playful intimacy tone better than a cold geometric sans. Figtree's soft, rounded geometry reads friendly. A serif (or a serif italic for level names only) could raise the "intimate/editorial" mood at the suggestive levels, but it is not required.
- Variable-font use to signal levels: shift weight and width subtly by level (for example lighter and more open for conversation, slightly bolder and tighter tracking for suggestive). This adds mood without new font files. Keep it subtle for legibility.
- Letter-spacing: use slight negative tracking (-0.01 to -0.02em) for large Bricolage display text, and 0 to +0.01em for small Figtree. Avoid wide-tracked all-caps for prompts. Use it only for tiny labels such as "LEVEL 2".
- Handwriting fonts: avoid them for prompts (legibility, dyslexia, dim light). At most, use one for a signature-style flourish or a "rules" card accent.

### Gaps
- The typefaces of Dipsea, Ferly and WNRS were not verified (fontsinuse.com was blocked, and no other source was found). Do not state specific fonts for these brands without checking.
- No peer-reviewed 2022–2026 study comparing serif vs sans for perceived intimacy or sensuality was found.

## 6. Illustration and art: abstract generative vs photos vs illustration; representation and inclusivity

### Takeaway
For sensual-but-safe content, the category trend is abstract, non-explicit and gender-neutral imagery (symbols, shapes, gradients), as in Maude's packaging. Abstract generative art per card avoids the representation and explicitness risks of photos and figurative illustration, and gives every couple room to project themselves.

### Cited Findings
- Maude's packaging uses "a collection of different symbols, designed to be abstract and not overtly sexual," explicitly moving away from the industry's "overt gender specific graphics." Its CEO framed the aim as serving "everyone, regardless of gender, age, or sexual preference." — [The Dieline: Maude](https://thedieline.com/maude-simplifies-sex-with-its-gender-inclusive-design/)
- Sexual-health app briefs targeting LGBTQ+ users ask for "personable, playful" illustration. Agency work exists for sensual illustrated identities for sexual-health apps. — [DesignCrowd brief](https://www.designcrowd.com/illustration-design/contest/4002842); [World Brand Design](https://worldbranddesign.com/?p=292885)
- Women's-wellness UI showcases commonly use pastel gradients and glassmorphism. — [Dribbble colour tag](https://dribbble.com/colors/fad5cc)
- OMGYES (sexual-wellness education) is noted for commissioned illustration over explicit imagery. (Search snippet only.) — [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/omgyes-illustrations/)

### Inferences
- Photos of real bodies or couples impose a body type, gender pairing, skin tone and age on every card. That excludes many couples and risks feeling explicit or stock-like. Avoid photos.
- Figurative illustration of people needs deliberate diversity (skin tones, body sizes, genders, disabilities, ages) and still risks a mismatch with any given couple. If used, keep figures stylised, faceless or partial (hands, silhouettes).
- Abstract generative art (gradients, organic shapes, textures, botanical or fluid forms) evokes warmth and sensuality without depicting anyone. It is inherently inclusive and safe for "suggestive, never graphic." Tie the palette to the level colour so art reinforces intensity. Set guardrails on the generator so it never produces bodies, faces or anatomical shapes, and moderate or seed it deterministically so both partners see identical art.
- The art must not compete with the text. Keep it on the card back (mystery side) or as a low-contrast background behind a scrim on the prompt side, so text contrast stays at 4.5:1 or more.

### Gaps
- No specific information on Ferly's, Dipsea's or Rosy's illustration systems was found.
- No research was found on user perceptions of AI-generated art in intimacy or wellness products. Perception risks around generative art (authenticity, "AI slop") are unexamined here.
