## Digital apps: features, device model, content levels, price, ratings, traction

### Takeaway
The market splits into four groups. (1) Dare/game apps such as Desire, Foreplay, Smush and Intimately Us are freemium and get "money grab" complaints. (2) Private yes/maybe/no matching apps such as Spicer, Kindu (possibly dead), Yes No Maybe, Cohesa and Couple App are the nearest match to the planned consent mechanic, but they swipe through lists rather than play a game in levels. (3) Relationship "wellness" apps such as Paired, Lovewick, Agapé and Lasting stick to conversation and charge $10-60+/yr. (4) Solo or audio sexual-wellness apps such as Dipsea, Quinn, Ferly and Coral cost $40-80/yr. Almost all are native apps that need both partners to install them, and most use subscriptions.

### Inferences
- Pricing benchmarks: impulse one-time unlocks at $4.99; packs at $1.99-5.99; subscriptions at $40-70/yr; lifetime options up to $399.99 (Dipsea). A web app avoids the 15-30% app store fee and can sell a one-time pass, which no matching app clearly offers.
- Every two-device competitor found is a native app that both partners must install and link (Spicer, Paired, Agapé, Coral, Smush, Couple App). The "partner has to install it too" friction is common to all of them, and packs sometimes have to be bought on both phones.
- Private-match consent is becoming common (Spicer, Cohesa, Couple App, Smush Fantasy Match, Yes No Maybe), so a hidden-No vote alone will not set the product apart. What does stand out is a three-way vote with a soft "not tonight" option, built into a staged, levelled game session rather than a fantasy-list swipe.

## Physical and card games: pricing, sales signals, why they spread

### Takeaway
We're Not Really Strangers (WNRS) is the category's breakout. It was built almost entirely on TikTok: about 3M followers by 2021, a $25 core game with $12 expansions, a Couples Edition the brand says has sold over 1M, sold mostly through its own shop and more recently TikTok Shop. Other decks such as the BestSelf Intimacy Deck sell at $21-30.

### Cited Findings
- WNRS was first published in Nov 2018 by photographer Koreen Odiney and run as a one-woman LA company. In 2021 it had about 3M TikTok followers and 90M+ likes. Most sales go through its own online shop. Core game US$25, expansions US$12. — [OMR](https://omr.com/en/daily/brand-playbook-tiktok); [OMR DE](https://omr.com/de/were-not-really-strangers-kartenspiel-tiktok-viral/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We%27re_Not_Really_Strangers)
- The TikTok account reached nearly 3M followers within about a year, driven by filmed play of the cards' emotionally raw questions. — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/?p=23430)
- The Couples Edition is claimed at "over 1 million sold" (brand claim). It expands the core game but is not needed to play. — [WNRS Couples Edition](https://www.werenotreallystrangers.com/pages/couples-edition)
- The WNRS Kids Edition reportedly sold 9,633 units for about US$229.8K on TikTok Shop between 23 Jun and 22 Jul 2026, mostly from creator content (single third-party report, unverified). — [TT123](https://www.tt123.com/kx/KXcuK1dt)
- Unofficial app clones exist on the App Store ("We Aren't Just Strangers - WRNS", "We're Not Really Strangers •"). — [appgoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6751618833/trends); [appgoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6744701678/trends)
- BestSelf Intimacy Deck: 150 cards (newer listings say 170), with a 50-card expansion. MSRP $30 (Faire wholesale), seen on sale around $21.24, and around €42 on international marketplaces. — [Faire](https://www.faire.com/product/p_4yu5nhfr74); [Refinery29 shop](https://www.refinery29.com/en-us/shop/product/couples-game-intimacy-deck-11280551); [Ubuy](https://www.ubuy.lu/en/product/157MF460Q-best-self-co-intimacy-deck-150-conversation-starter-intimacy-cards-for-couples)

### Inferences
- Card games spread because playing them makes good video. Couples film the reactions, which works as free UGC marketing. A two-phone app could build in a "shareable but not exposing" moment, for example a level-complete card that reveals nothing private.
- Physical decks set a one-time price of about $20-30 that Gen Z accepts, which is evidence that a one-time digital purchase in the $5-15 range would seem fair compared with subscriptions.

## Recurring user complaints and praise

### Takeaway
The most common complaint is a paywall that hollows out the free tier, together with pricing that feels nickel-and-dime (Desire, Lovewick, Paired, Spicer). After that come regional price unfairness, unclear "does one subscription cover both partners", poor fit for non-monogamous and queer couples, and slow support. Users praise privacy (hidden No answers), playful tone, and research-backed content.

### Cited Findings

## Gaps in the market a consent-first two-phone game could own

### Takeaway
No competitor found combines all of these: (a) a no-install web app on two phones, (b) a staged, levelled session that climbs from conversation to sensual, (c) a three-option private vote that includes a soft "not tonight", and (d) fair one-time or low global pricing aimed at Gen Z. Each exists separately in a different product.

### Cited Findings
- Two-phone pairing with invite codes exists in native apps only (Smush, Spicer, Paired, Agapé) — [Smush](https://www.smushapp.com/couples-game-app); [App Store Spicer](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1335558932).
- Couple App has no Android version, which cuts out mixed iOS/Android couples — [trycoupleapp](https://trycoupleapp.com/blogs/best-couple-apps-2026).
- Graduated intensity exists as separate game modes (Smush "soft to wild") or comfort toggles (Intimately Us), not as a progressive levelled session with per-step consent — [Smush](https://www.smushapp.com/couples-game-app); [Date Your Spouse](https://dateyourspouse.com/intimately-us-app-for-couples/).
- Matching apps use Yes/Maybe/No on a static list of ideas (Spicer, Kindu). None found frames the third option as "not tonight" (a non-permanent no) — [App Store Spicer](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1335558932); [DatingAdvice Kindu](https://www.datingadvice.com/for-women/kindu-app-builds-intimacy-with-sexy-questions-and-provocative-activities).

### Inferences
- Differentiators to own: (1) a zero-install link the partner opens in the browser, which removes the "partner doesn't have the app" barrier and the double-purchase complaint; (2) one purchase covers both partners; (3) a "not tonight" vote that is hidden, temporary and carries no penalty, so a partner who is less keen can take part without pressure; (4) inclusive, non-gendered prompts from the start; (5) a no-account or minimal-data privacy model (stealth and no-data claims are already being marketed); (6) purchasing-power-adjusted global pricing; (7) a tone that is sensual and never graphic, between the clean faith apps and explicit dare apps; (8) shareable "level complete" moments for TikTok, in the way WNRS spread.
- Risks: matching mechanics are easy to copy and many clones appeared in 2025-2026. Web apps lose app-store discoverability, so distribution would depend on TikTok and creators.

### Gaps
- I could not verify whether any 2026 web-based (no-install) couples intimacy game exists, because searches surfaced native apps only.
