Research date: 14 November 2026. **Method warning for the report writer:** this session could run web searches but could not open any page (every direct fetch was blocked by the session's network settings, and the Ahrefs keyword and traffic-by-country tools returned "Insufficient plan"). Every figure below therefore comes from search-result summaries of the linked page, not from a full read. Figures I regard as weak are marked. ## 1. Which countries spend the most on couples/relationship and sexual-wellness apps and products?

### Takeaway
The United States is far ahead on every spending measure found (app spend, dating-app spend, sexual-wellness spend), and Indian app users spend roughly one-hundredth as much per download as US users. No public per-country revenue split exists for Paired, Lovewick, Desire or Coral, so the ranking below the US rests on general app-spend and market-size proxies.

### Inferences
- My arithmetic, not a published figure: $345M over 6.6B downloads gives about $0.05 per download for India in Q2 2026, against about $4.60 in the US, a gap of roughly 90 to 1. Using the $0.03 figure the gap is roughly 150 to 1. Either way India pays about 1% of the US rate per install.
- My arithmetic on the only two comparable sexual-wellness estimates (different publishers, so indicative only): the US $8.2B and India $1.48B, divided by populations of roughly 340M and 1.45B (population figures not sourced in this research), give about $24 per person in the US against about $1 in India.
- The Nordic and UK ownership finding suggests that openness to sexual-wellness products is broadly similar across north-west Europe, so market size there is set by population and language, not attitudes.
- The US share of global in-app spending is about a third on these numbers ($55.5B to $59B of $167B, my arithmetic), which makes it the only country that could carry the product alone.

### Gaps
- No country-level revenue or download split for Paired, Lovewick, Desire or Coral was found in any free source. Similarweb and AppTail have per-country pages but no readable figures; Sensor Tower, Appfigures and data.ai country data need a paid seat. The Similarweb connector in this session failed to connect.
- No 2025 or 2026 country ranking table for app spending beyond the US, China and India was visible (the State of Mobile 2026 "Top 20 markets" chart exists but its values were not in any snippet), so UK, Germany, Canada and Australia app-spend figures are missing.
- No reliable sexual-wellness market size for the UK, Canada, Australia, Netherlands, Nordics, Ireland, New Zealand or Singapore, and no published per-capita figure for any country.
- No dating or relationship category revenue by country for 2025 as a single year.

## 3. English proficiency and willingness to pay for the main candidates

### Takeaway
An English-only game is fully usable in the six native-English markets and Singapore, and well understood in the Netherlands, Germany and the Nordics, which all sit in the top "very high" band of the 2025 EF index. Willingness to pay tracks app spend per download, where the US and other rich markets sit about two orders of magnitude above India.

### Cited Findings
- EF English Proficiency Index 2025 (published 19 November 2025, 123 countries and territories, non-native speakers only): Netherlands first at 624; Croatia second at 617; Germany fourth at 615 (up from tenth); Norway 613; Portugal 612; Denmark 611; Sweden 609; Belgium ninth. All are in the "very high proficiency" band. — [EF press release](https://www.ef.com/ca/about-us/press/articles/2025/ef-english-proficiency-index-2025-launched/); [Wikipedia table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EF_English_Proficiency_Index); [Croatia Week](https://www.croatiaweek.com/croatia-climbs-to-2nd-in-the-world-for-english-language-proficiency/); [IamExpat Germany](https://www.iamexpat.de/expat-info/germany-news/how-good-germanys-english-2025)
- Singapore was removed from the 2025 index because EF reclassified it as a native English-speaking country. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EF_English_Proficiency_Index)
- Malaysia ranks highest among the Asian markets studied in 2025. — [Human Resources Online](https://production.humanresourcesonline.net/2025-ranking-english-proficiency-levels-around-asia)
- A different page lists Germany at 598 and the Netherlands at 636; these look like 2024-edition figures and conflict with the 2025 numbers above. — [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/country-rankings/english-proficiency-by-country)
- Willingness to pay proxies: revenue per download about $4.60 in the US against about $0.03 in India and over $0.20 in Southeast Asia and Latin America (see section 1 for dates). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/india-is-starting-to-pay-for-apps-not-just-download-them/); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/india-app-downloads-revenue-gap-sensor-tower-report-126042400618_1.html)
- India is starting to pay: record $345M in Q2 2026, up 35% year on year, while US app revenue fell 3% in the same quarter. — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/india-mobile-app-market-q2-2026)

### Inferences
- The EF index measures self-selected test takers, so it overstates what a whole population can do. A suggestive game depends on nuance and humour, so "very high" on EF does not mean a German or Dutch couple would choose English for an intimate evening. Local-language competitors will have an edge there.
- For an English-only launch, the practical order by language fit is: US, UK, Canada (outside Quebec), Australia, Ireland, New Zealand, Singapore, then Netherlands and the Nordics, then Germany.
- India's English-using population is large in absolute terms but the paying share is small; growth of 35% a year from a low base does not change the ranking in the next one to two years.

### Gaps
- India's 2025 EF rank and score could not be confirmed (one undated mirror shows 484, "low proficiency"; do not cite it as 2025).
- No per-country revenue per download for the UK, Canada, Australia, Germany, Netherlands, Nordics, Ireland, New Zealand or Singapore was found.
- No survey of willingness to pay for relationship or couples apps by country was found.
- Finland's 2025 score was not captured.

## 4. Legal and payment friction per country

### Takeaway
The main Western markets regulate pornography, not suggestive couples content, so a "suggestive at most" game sits outside mandatory age checks in the UK, most US states and Australia, provided the content rule holds. India carries the highest legal and payment friction of the candidates: obscenity enforcement tightened in 2025 and Stripe remains invite-only there.

### Cited Findings
- UK: sites hosting pornography must use highly effective age assurance under the Online Safety Act; enforcement expanded in July 2025. Ofcom fines to date include £800,000 (Kick Online Entertainment, 12 Feb 2026), £1.35M plus £50,000 (8579 LLC, 23 Feb 2026, largest so far), £450,000 (4chan, 19 Mar 2026), £600,000 (Youngtek Solutions) and £630,000 in total (Fapello, 9 Jul 2026). — [Ofcom, Kick](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/ofcom-fines-porn-company-800k-for-failing-to-introduce-age-checks); [Ofcom, 8579 LLC](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/ofcom-fines-porn-company-1.35-million-for-not-having-age-checks); [Ofcom, 4chan](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/4chan-fined-450000-for-not-protecting-children-from-online-pornography); [Ofcom, Fapello](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/ofcom-fines-porn-company-630000-for-age-check-failings); [RPC, autumn 2026](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/technology-digital/autumn-2026/online-safety-act-ofcom-fines-two-adult-sites-for-age-verification-failures/)
- UK: Ofcom treated a site that geoblocked UK users as still in scope, so blocking the UK is not a clean exit. — [Biometric Update, Aug 2026](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202608/ofcom-fines-geoblocked-porn-site-signaling-tougher-age-assurance-enforcement)
- UK: about 77 to 80 of the top 100 pornography sites used in the UK had age checks by early 2026. — [Inforrm, 11 Mar 2026](https://inforrm.org/2026/03/11/ofcom-steps-up-online-safety-act-enforcement-with-two-further-age-assurance-fines-for-pornographic-platforms-alexandros-antoniou/)
- US: about 27 states require age verification for adult sites as of August to September 2026, none currently blocked by a court; trackers range from 25 to 29 depending on definition. — [RecordingLaw, Aug 2026](https://www.recordinglaw.com/us-laws/age-verification-laws/); [AVPA](https://avpassociation.com/us-state-age-verification-laws-for-adult-content/); [Mayer Brown, Aug 2026](https://mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/childrens-privacy-mid-year-update-key-legislative-developments-and-emerging-trends-for-businesses)
- US: the Texas model (HB 1181) applies only where more than one-third of a website's material is "sexual material harmful to minors", a definition built on the Miller obscenity test adapted for minors. The Supreme Court upheld it on 27 June 2025 in Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton. — [Texas Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code 129B.002](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._civ._practice_&_remedies_code_section_129b.002); [Texas Legislature bill text](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/html/HB01181E.htm); [Super Lawyers](https://www.superlawyers.com/resources/internet/age-verification-laws-accessing-adult-content/)
- US: a separate family of laws covers social media and app stores; 21 states had passed social-media laws on minors by July 2026. — [Mayer Brown, Aug 2026](https://mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/childrens-privacy-mid-year-update-key-legislative-developments-and-emerging-trends-for-businesses)
- Australia: the Age-Restricted Material Codes are in force and require pornography sites, app stores, AI chatbots and other platforms to confirm users are 18 or over before giving access to adult or high-impact content. One set was registered on 27 June 2025 and another on 9 September 2025, with age-assurance measures due by about 27 June 2026 and 9 September 2026. — [eSafety media release](https://esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/online-safety-codes-introduce-real-world-protections-for-children-online); [Baker McKenzie, Sep 2025](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/alerts/2025/09/australia-phase-2-online-safety-codes-registered-by-esafety-commissioner); [eSafety regulatory guidance, Dec 2025](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/OnlineSafetyCodesandStandardsRegulatoryGuidanceDec2025.pdf); [ID Tech Wire](https://idtechwire.com/australias-age-verification-rules-for-adult-content-take-effect-prompting-pushback/)
- Germany: age checks for adult content have been a legal obligation since 2002 (JMStV, KJM criteria). — [Free Speech Coalition, global policies](https://action.freespeechcoalition.com/age-verification-resources/global-age-verification-policies/)
- India: on 25 July 2025 the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting ordered ISPs to block 25 streaming apps and sites (including ULLU and ALTT) for obscene content, citing IT Act sections 67 and 67A and related provisions; an earlier action in March 2024 blocked 18 platforms. Press described the blocked content as "soft porn", not hardcore. — [Business Today, 25 Jul 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/govt-bans-25-ott-apps-websites-for-streaming-soft-porn-content-486300-2025-07-25); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/tech/india-blocks-25-ott-platforms-for-streaming-obscene-content-under-it-act); [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/news/govt-bans-ott-platforms-ullu-altt-desiflix-for-soft-porn-content)
- India payments: Stripe says it offers services in India by invite only and supports a select number of businesses. — [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/accepting-international-payments-from-stripe-accounts-in-india)
- India payments: third-party analysis argues global merchants on Stripe lose Indian customers at checkout (vendor source with a competing product; no decline rate visible). — [EximPe](https://eximpe.com/blog/psp/stripe-for-india-what-works-what-doesnt-and-where-global-merchants-lose-indian-customers)
- Stripe operates in 40+ countries and 135+ currencies as of 2026 (third-party blog, unverified against Stripe's own list). — [CS-Cart](https://www.cs-cart.com/blog/stripe-supported-countries/)

### Inferences
- The India block list shows the authorities act against content well short of explicit pornography. A suggestive adult couples game with a name containing "After Dark" is closer to that line in India than in any other candidate market. This supports the founder's view on legal risk.
- For a non-Indian Stripe account, Indian buyers would pay by international card, which adds a second barrier on top of low spend. I found no decline-rate figure, so the size of this barrier is unquantified.
- The US one-third threshold is about the share of a site's content that is harmful to minors under a Miller-style test. A site with no explicit depictions should fall outside it, but the prurient-interest prong is not limited to explicit material on its face, so this is a counsel question, not a settled point. No source found addresses suggestive text-only content.
- Australia's wording ("adult or high-impact content") is broader than the UK's pornography test. The earlier report's advice to keep a written below-class-2 rationale matters more now that the codes are in force.
- Friction ranking from lowest to highest on this evidence: Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, Netherlands, Nordics and Singapore (nothing specific found), then the US (state patchwork aimed at pornography), UK (active regulator, pornography test), Germany (long-standing strict youth-protection regime), Australia (broadest wording, new codes), then India.

### Gaps
- Nothing specific was found for Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, the Netherlands, the Nordics or Singapore. "Nothing found" is not the same as "no rules"; Singapore in particular has content-classification rules that were not researched.
- No 2025 or 2026 change to Germany's JMStV was confirmed.
- The exact Online Safety Act definition of pornographic content was not re-verified here (the earlier report cites it).
- No Stripe restricted-business change since the earlier report was checked, and Stripe's official country list was not read.
- No Indian enforcement action against a text-only or card-based couples product was found, in either direction.
- RBI rules on recurring card payments were not researched; they matter less for a one-time pass.

## 5. Which domain endings do users in the top countries trust?

### Takeaway
.com is the safe default for the US and for a multi-country English brand; country endings are strongly preferred by local shoppers in Germany, the Netherlands, Australia and the UK, but nearly all of that evidence is funded by the registries that sell those endings. No 2023 to 2026 survey was found that tests new endings such as .app, .game or .love.

### Cited Findings
- Germany: in a December 2025 Ipsos survey, 72% chose .de when free to choose, against 29% for .com (multiple answers appear to have been allowed). Reported in a press release from a DENIC member company, so industry-funded. — [PresseBox / Secura](https://www.pressebox.de/presscorner/firma/secura/meldung/boxid/1304785/iframe/839)
- Netherlands: shown similar webshop names with different endings, 82% chose .nl (SIDN with GfK, undated). In a 2022 SIDN/GfK study 74% preferred a .nl webshop for a major purchase. In an SIDN panel, 89% said .nl was their first choice for goods and local outings, while 23% would choose .com for travel. All registry-funded. — [SIDN, why .nl](https://sidn.nl/en/nl-domain-name/why-opt-for-a-nl-domain-name); [SIDN, webshop trust](https://www.sidn.nl/en/news-and-blogs/good-design-and-a-suitable-domain-name-promote-trust-in-webshops); [SIDN, brand preferences](https://www.sidn.nl/en/news-and-blogs/domain-name-extension-brand-preferences-remain-stable)
- Netherlands: among registrants in 2025, 67% preferred .nl and 26% .com (registry data on businesses registering names, not consumers). — [SIDN, 2025 review](https://www.sidn.nl/en/news-and-blogs/the-nl-domain-in-2025-a-year-of-contraction-and-change)
- Netherlands: a July 2026 article argues .com can hold a brand back in the Dutch market and recommends a .nl site with .com redirecting to it. It is listed as partner (sponsored) content and contains no survey. — [NL Times, 13 Jul 2026](https://nltimes.nl/2026/07/13/com-may-holding-back-dutch-market)
- Australia: auDA's "Why .au" research (2024 fieldwork, more than 2,100 consumers and small businesses, published 13 February 2025) found three in four consumers are more likely to trust an Australian business whose site ends in .au, and half will only buy online from a business with a .au site. Registry-funded. — [auDA](https://www.auda.org.au/news-insights/statements/australians-seek-au-online-as-a-marker-of-trust/)
- UK: 70% of consumers ranked a .UK ending as their first preference when buying online (2021 Yonder survey for Nominet; registry-funded and older than the 2023 to 2026 window). — [The UK Domain infographic](https://theukdomain.uk/5-great-reasons-renewal-infographic/)
- UK: 70.5% of 1,000 UK respondents trust .co.uk and .com more than alternatives (survey by the agency Varn; date not confirmed, believed older). — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/new-research-visitors-don-t-trust-alternative-domain-extensions/)
- Global baseline: ICANN's 2015 consumer survey found 94% trust for geographically targeted endings (75% to 98% by country) against 90% for legacy endings, and higher acceptance of new endings outside the US and Europe. Rigorous but eleven years old. — [ICANN global consumer survey, May 2015](https://newgtlds.icann.org/en/reviews/cct/global-consumer-survey-29may15-en.pdf)
- US: 80% of US consumers avoid websites with oddly spelled domain names (GoDaddy 2025 Consumer Pulse; registrar-funded; about spelling, not endings). — [Strategic Revenue](https://www.strategicrevenue.com/godaddy-consumer-survey-says-your-domain-name-still-matters-in-2025/)

### Inferences
- Most registry surveys ask about buying from a local shop. A free browser game reached through a shared link or a TikTok bio is a different decision, so these numbers probably overstate how much a country ending matters for this product. They do show there is no penalty for .com in the US, and no evidence anywhere that a new ending is trusted more than .com.
- For an English-only product aimed first at the US, UK, Canada and Australia, one .com serves all four. A .co.uk or .com.au redirect is cheap insurance; a .de or .nl only earns its keep if the game is translated.
- The registry data implies a brand name that is short, plainly spelled and free of sexual words carries more trust than any choice of ending, consistent with the GoDaddy finding on odd spellings.
- If the ranking in section 6 is accepted, the brand need not be tested for meaning in Hindi or priced for India at launch, though a quick check that the name is inoffensive there costs little.

### Gaps
- No 2023 to 2026 consumer survey on trust in .app, .game, .love, .io or .co was found. The GrowthBadger study the earlier report cites is the latest known and predates this window.
- No independent (non-registry) survey for Germany, the Netherlands, Australia or the UK, and nothing at all for Canada (.ca), Ireland (.ie), New Zealand (.nz), Singapore (.sg), the Nordics or India (.in).
- No click-through data by ending (search or social) for 2023 to 2026 was found.
- The Ipsos .de sample size and question wording were not visible.

## 6. Overall ranking by commercial potential, and the India question

### Takeaway
On the evidence found, the order for an English-language, pay-once browser game is: United States first by a wide margin, then the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia, then Germany, the Netherlands and the Nordics as English-capable secondary markets, then Ireland, New Zealand and Singapore as small but easy ones. The founder's view on India is confirmed for revenue and legal risk, with one correction: India is growing fastest and is useful for free reach, not for sales.

### Inferences
Proposed ranking (my judgement from the proxies above; no source ranks countries for this product):

| Rank | Country | Why | Main drag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | United States | About a third of world app spend; largest dating and sexual-wellness market; about 30% of "couples games" searches; .com is native | State age-verification patchwork (aimed at pornography) |
| 2 | United Kingdom | Native English, high spend, similar sex-toy ownership to Nordics, .com trusted alongside .co.uk | Active regulator; keep the "not pornographic" assessment current |
| 3 | Canada | Native English, US-like spending and culture, no specific friction found | Smaller; no country data found |
| 4 | Australia | Native English, high spend | Broadest age-restricted wording now in force; half of shoppers say they buy only from .au sites (registry survey) |
| 5 | Germany | Largest rich non-English market, English score 615 | English-only limits intimacy use; strong .de preference; strict youth-protection regime |
| 6 | Netherlands | Best non-native English (624) | Small; strong .nl preference |
| 7 | Nordics (Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland) | Very high English, sex-toy ownership above half | Four small markets |
| 8 | Ireland | Native English, no friction found | Very small |
| 9 | New Zealand | Native English, no friction found | Very small |
| 10 | Singapore | Native-level English, wealthy | Very small; content rules not researched |
| Low | India | Huge reach, fastest growth | About 1% of US revenue per download; obscenity enforcement; Stripe invite-only |

- The top four share one language and one trusted ending, so the domain and name should be chosen for US, UK, Canadian and Australian ears first: a .com, plain spelling, no sexual words.
- India verdict: the founder's low-priority call is supported on both counts he named. The correction is on trend, not level: Indian spending is growing 35% a year and the earlier reports show real cultural demand, so India is worth leaving open (no geo-block, toned-down content) and revisiting in a year or two, not worth shaping the brand or domain around.
- Positions 2 to 4 are close and could swap with real per-country data; positions 5 to 7 depend on whether the game is ever translated.

### Gaps
- The ranking is built on general app-spend, language and legal proxies. No direct evidence of couples-app revenue by country was obtainable, so the order below the US is a judgement.
- Population and income figures used informally for ranks 5 to 10 were not sourced in this research.
- Philippines, South Africa, Malaysia and Nigeria (large English-using markets with low spend) were not assessed beyond one mention each.
- To firm this up: one paid Sensor Tower or Appfigures export for Paired by country, and a manual Google Trends interest-by-region check for the five search terms.
