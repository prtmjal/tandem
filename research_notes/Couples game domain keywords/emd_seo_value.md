# EMD SEO value and domain best practices for a couples intimacy game (18+, suggestive, US + India)

Method note: research was done 2026-11-05 using web search. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (seroundtable.com, growthbadger.com, semrush.com did not resolve), so most findings come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not full-text reads. Quotes should be spot-checked against the originals before publication. Several key Google statements are older (2012-2022); they are flagged where relevant.

## 1. Do exact match domains (EMDs) still help SEO in 2025-2026? Google statements and studies

### Takeaway
No. Google's consistent public line (Mueller, Illyes) is that keywords in a domain give no ranking bonus. The 2012 EMD update demoted low-quality EMDs, and later correlation studies point to authority, links and brand mentions instead. The only residual benefits are indirect: possible CTR and relevance cues to humans, and keyword-rich anchor text when people link using the bare brand name. For AI Overviews and LLM answers, branded web mentions matter more than the domain string.

### Cited Findings
- The EMD update launched about 27-28 Sept 2012. Matt Cutts announced it on Twitter as reducing "low-quality exact-match domains" in results, affecting 0.6% of English-US queries, separate from Panda and Penguin. **Older (2012).** [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-emd-update-15776.html); [SEJ algorithm history](https://searchenginejournal.com/google-algorithm-history/emd-update)
- A Panda update (about 2.4% of queries) shipped at the same time, so many sites could not tell which change had hit them. Moz tracking reportedly saw about 3.6% of monitored top-10 terms shift. **Older (2012).** [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-two-updates-15782.html); [Search Engine People](https://searchenginepeople.com/blog/emd-update-minor.html)
- The update targeted thin-content EMDs, not EMDs as such. Strong EMD sites were not the intended target. [Sistrix](https://www.sistrix.com/?p=32116); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/do-exact-match-domains-still-work/)
- Mueller: "There's no secret (or public) SEO-bonus for having your keywords in the domain name." [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/keyword-domains-seo)
- Mueller (Reddit, as reported): "I'm not a fan of keyword-keyword domains, but YMMV." He also said: "If you're planning on using this for the long run, I would not recommend using just a keyword domain name." He favours a unique brand name that can survive changes in the business model. [SEJ, 2024: Mueller on keyword domain names and branding](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/john-mueller-on-keyword-domain-names-and-branding-for-seo/504423/); [Domain Gang](https://domaingang.com/domain-news/keyword-domains-seo-thread-on-reddit-got-deleted-heres-what-google-geek-said)
- Mueller (Mastodon, as reported) said a domain name will never make or break your SEO. [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-your-domain-will-never-make-or-break-your-seo-35875.html)
- Mueller said an existing site won't drop in rankings just because it is an EMD. Drops have other causes. [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/amp/google-rankings-emd-34932.html)
- Mueller responded to an EMD ("christmas trees") that did not rank: having the keywords in the domain doesn't make it rank for those keywords. [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-exact-match-domain-not-ranking-30406.html)
- Mueller advised picking a "reasonable site name" to rank for in search, i.e. a distinctive brand name that people can search for. [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-reasonable-site-name-seo-rank-40660.html)
- Gary Illyes (Search Off the Record podcast, as reported): keyword-rich domains "don't really help much" for rankings, and a domain is a business decision. Since site owners can manipulate URL content, Google likely doesn't want to put much weight on it. Keywords "can be helpful for CTR sometimes". [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-on-top-level-domains-for-seo-35752.html); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-says-url-keywords-become-irrelevant-once-a-page-has-content/)
- Illyes (2016): keywords in new gTLDs (keyword TLDs) do not improve rankings, and migrating to a keyword TLD won't help. **Older (2016).** [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ignores-content-in-tlds-22236.html); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-migrating-to-a-keyword-tld-wont-improve-rankings-22201.html)
- In Backlinko's study of 11.8M Google results (Ahrefs data), domain authority (Ahrefs DR) and referring domains correlate with rankings. Keyword-in-title showed near-zero correlation. The study gives no positive EMD signal and is correlation only. **Dated (about 2020, still referenced).** [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/search-engine-ranking)
- On AI search, Ahrefs (Dec 2025, 75k brands, as summarised) found branded web mentions and YouTube mentions were the strongest predictors of AI-platform citations, with backlinks weakly correlated. Ahrefs also reported that 76% of AI Overview citations come from pages in Google's top 10. [Summary: myamazonguy.com](https://myamazonguy.com/news/brand-mentions-in-ai-search-platforms/); [shno.co roundup](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/brand-mentions-statistics)
- Semrush and Kevin Indig (2026, 3,981 domains) found 61.7% of AI citations are "ghost citations": the engine links to a page without naming the brand in the answer. A memorable brand name that people search directly therefore matters. [SEJ](https://searchenginejournal.com/the-ghost-citation-problem/572453); [Growth Memo](https://www.growth-memo.com/p/the-ghost-citation-problem)

### Inferences
- An EMD like "couplesgame.com" or "intimacygame.com" would give approximately zero direct ranking advantage in 2026. It could carry a small CTR or relevance cue on SERPs and some keyword anchor text from natural links.
- In AI Overviews and LLM search, the domain string is not a citation factor. Distinct branded mentions are, and a generic EMD is harder to build distinct mentions for, because "couples game" mentions are not attributable to one brand.
- The 2012 EMD filter targets thin EMD sites. A quality site on a partial-match domain is not at risk, though an EMD sets expectations of generic content.

### Gaps
- No 2023-2026 independent large-scale study isolating EMD/PMD ranking correlation was found (Moz's EMD correlation work dates to 2011-2013). Vendor 2025 claims such as "30% traffic drops" for EMDs (WebProNews) cite no primary data and should be ignored.
- No measured data on EMD CTR uplift exists beyond Illyes's qualitative "sometimes".

## 2. Partial-match domains, hyphens, length, and TLD choice (.com / .app / .co / .in / .io)

### Takeaway
A short, pronounceable brand word, optionally plus one generic word (e.g. "[brand]game", "play[brand]"), on .com is the safest default. .app is a credible second choice for an app product and enforces HTTPS by browser preload. .co trails .com in trust and recall, and .io carries a developer connotation. For India, .in can be held as a defensive redirect. Avoid hyphens and keyword strings. No reliable CTR study for .app exists.

### Cited Findings
- GrowthBadger survey (1,500 people, 8 TLDs, not including .app): .com had the highest trust, with .co next at 3.4. .biz was lowest at 2.9 (17% below .com). Recall was correct 44% of the time for .com vs 33% for .co. It measured trust and recall, not clicks. **Undated in summary, about 2019-2020.** [GrowthBadger](https://growthbadger.com/top-level-domains/); [PDF](https://growthbadger.com/wp-content/uploads/GrowthBadger-domain-extensions-experiment-TLD-study.pdf)
- A Semrush-published survey reported that visitors trust alternative extensions less than .com. Details were unreadable this session. [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/new-research-visitors-don-t-trust-alternative-domain-extensions/)
- A 2016 UK survey found 70.5% trust .co.uk/.com over alternatives. **Older, UK-only.** [Varn](https://varn.co.uk/?p=4862)
- Google: keyword or new gTLDs get no ranking boost, and TLD choice is not a ranking lever (Illyes, 2016). [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ignores-content-in-tlds-22236.html)
- .com scale: 157.9M .com names (end Q2 2025) of 371.7M total, and 161.0M by Q4 2025 (386.9M total). .com remains the default name space, which also means scarcity for short names. [Verisign/DNIB via realtimeregister](https://realtimeregister.com/blogs/key-insights-from-the-q2-2025-domain-name-industry-brief-domain-name-market-keeps-climbing); [Q4 2025 report summary](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/2609097587)
- .app is run by Google Registry and is HSTS-preloaded at the TLD level, so browsers will only load .app sites over HTTPS and no HTTP fallback is possible. Typical cost is $12-20/yr, with registry premiums on some names. [Comodo SSL Store](https://comodosslstore.com/blog/google-launches-app-top-level-domain-with-hsts-as-a-default.html); [EuroDNS](https://www.eurodns.com/blog/hsts-preload-list-site-security); [INWX](https://www.inwx.ch/en/APP-domain); [Oh Dear case](https://ohdear.app/blog/were-a-featured-app-domain-on-googles-registry)
- One vendor claim says "a 2024 study" found 38% higher CTR for established TLDs. No primary study was traceable, so treat it as unverified. [dev.to](https://dev.to/julianneagu/app-vs-com-domains-the-complete-saas-founders-decision-framework-3hm0)

### Inferences
- The HSTS requirement on .app is a non-issue for this project, which already deploys on Render/Railway/Fly with TLS. It does mean local or staging hosts cannot use plain HTTP on the .app name.
- For US plus India, .com is understood in both markets. .in or .co.in adds local trust signals for India but is not needed for ranking. Google uses ccTLDs for geotargeting, so a .in primary would weaken US relevance. Hold .in as a redirect.
- Hyphens and long keyword strings hurt the "radio test" (spoken word-of-mouth) and look spammy. Keep the name to 2 syllables-ish, at most 2 words, with no hyphens or numbers.
- .co is commonly mistyped as .com, so traffic can leak to whoever owns the .com.

### Gaps
- No primary CTR data compares .app to .com. No India-specific TLD trust survey was found. No reliable data on hyphen CTR penalties was found (common advice, uncited).

## 3. Risks of sexual words in the domain ("sex", "dirty", "naughty", "spicy")

### Takeaway
Explicit words in the domain create compounding risk across ad review, payments, app-store metadata, network filters and SafeSearch, for no SEO upside. "Sex" is the highest-risk word. "Dirty" and "naughty" are adult-coded. "Spicy" is softer but still flags review. A neutral or romantic brand keeps every channel open, and the suggestive positioning can live in copy that is easy to tone per channel.

### Cited Findings
- **Google Ads:** Google can judge a destination from the ad, the website, the account and third-party sources together. Sexual content ads may run on Search but not on Display, AdMob, Ad Manager or YouTube. The 2021 policy restructure restricts "dating with sexually suggestive elements". [Google Ads sexual content policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6023699?hl=en); [Search Engine Roundtable on the 2021 change](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ads-adult-policy-sexually-suggestive-30850.html)
- Google added a dating and companionship ads policy with mandatory advertiser certification (March 2025). It prohibits livestream or chat apps with sexually suggestive content, among other things. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15327789?hl=en-AU); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ads-policy-for-dating-companionship-38519.html)
- **Meta:** sexual health products are allowed only if focused on health and medical efficacy, not "sexual pleasure or enhancement", and must target 18+. Adult content and "sexually explicit language" are prohibited in ads. [Meta Health & Wellness standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness); [Meta Adult sexual solicitation and sexually explicit language](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/adult-sexual-solicitation-and-sexually-explicit-language/)
- A 2025 Center for Intimacy Justice report alleged Meta inconsistently rejects intimacy-adjacent ads as "adult products", which suggests unpredictable enforcement. [Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/facebook-and-instagram-accused-of-rejecting-women-s-health-ads)
- **Stripe:** it cannot work with businesses that sell "adult content or services", including "pornography and other mature audience content (including literature, imagery and other media) designed for the purpose of sexual gratification". The list was last updated Feb 2025 and is not exhaustive. [Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/en-gb-ca/legal/restricted-businesses); [Stripe FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/prohibited-and-restricted-business-list-faqs)
- **App stores:** Apple app names are capped at 30 characters. Guideline 2.3 bars stuffing metadata with irrelevant terms, and 2.3.8 requires metadata (icons, screenshots, previews) to be appropriate for all audiences (4+) regardless of the app's age rating. Google Play caps titles at 30 characters and prohibits sexually suggestive content in store metadata. [Phiture ASO guide](https://phiture.com/asostack/app-store-and-play-store-guidelines/); [Google Play metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en); [ConductAtlas on Apple 2.3](https://conductatlas.com/platform/apple/apple-app-store-review-guidelines/provision/CA-P-018839/app-metadata-must-not-include-irrelevant-trademarked-terms/)
- **SafeSearch and filters:** Edelman's Harvard study found SafeSearch overblocked tens of thousands of non-explicit pages, including education, government and news sites. **Older (2003).** [Harvard Berkman](https://cyber.harvard.edu/archived_content/people/edelman/google-safesearch). ICANN list posts discuss domain strings containing "sex" being caught. [ICANN GNSO archive](https://gnso.icann.org/mailing-lists/archives/ga/msg01110.html). Corporate and DNS filters block by category lists. [DNSFilter categories](https://help.dnsfilter.com/hc/en-us/articles/29593839153171-Filtering-Policy-content-categories); [Cisco Umbrella SafeSearch](https://docs.sse.cisco.com/sse-dns-guide/docs/what-is-safesearch)
- Google applies SafeSearch filtering at URL level, not only site-wide. [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-safe-search-filter-url-24090.html)

### Inferences
- The domain is part of every review surface: the ad's display URL, the Stripe account's business URL, the app-store support URL, and the share-link preview. An explicit word there biases human reviewers toward "adult" classification before they read the product. For Stripe this matters most, because the game sells a Couple Pass, and an "adult content" classification could close the account.
- Apple's 4+ metadata rule means a store name containing "Sex" or "Dirty" would likely be rejected even for a 17+ app. The web domain and app name should match for brand recall, so the domain should pass that bar too.
- Category filters (workplace, school, some ISPs and parental controls in India) categorise by content and lists. Keyword-in-domain heuristics exist in some products but are not documented by vendors. Assume "sex" and "naughty" in a hostname raise the odds of blocking, and of the link being collapsed or warned on in social link previews. This is an inference: link-preview behaviour was not documented in sources found.
- Product fit: the game is "suggestive at most, never graphic", so an explicit domain would also misrepresent it, and attract the wrong search intent (porn-adjacent queries) that SafeSearch filters out anyway.

### Gaps
- TikTok ad policy on sexual wellness and suggestive apps was not retrieved (search returned nothing). Verify TikTok's "Adult products and services" policy directly.
- No vendor documentation was found showing that specific corporate filters block on the substring "sex" in a domain. No data was found on WhatsApp, iMessage or X link-preview suppression for adult-word domains.
- India-specific rules (e.g. IT Rules 2021, MeitY blocking practice) as they apply to domain names were not researched.

## 4. Brandability: memorability, trademarkability (USPTO and India), word-of-mouth

### Takeaway
Generic or descriptive names ("Couples Game", "Intimacy Cards") are weak or unregistrable trademarks in both the US and India, and hard to own in search and AI answers. A suggestive or arbitrary coined brand is registrable, defensible and memorable. Pair it with a descriptive tagline, not a descriptive domain.

### Cited Findings
- **USPTO:** TMEP §1209 governs refusals for marks that are "merely descriptive" under 15 U.S.C. §1052(e)(1). Descriptive marks immediately convey a feature, quality or purpose of the goods. Suggestive marks need "imagination, thought, and perception" to connect to the goods and are registrable. Doubt goes to the applicant (In re Remacle). [BitLaw TMEP 1209](https://www.bitlaw.com/source/tmep/1209.html); [Patent Trademark Blog](https://patenttrademarkblog.com/?p=746); [USPTO glossary](https://uspto.report/resources/glossary/descriptive-mark/)
- Arguing "suggestive not descriptive" commonly fails unless the applicant explains the mental leap consumers must make. [GleanMark](https://gleanmark.com/insights/descriptiveness-office-action-strategies)
- **India:** Trade Marks Act 1999 s.9(1)(b) refuses marks that "designate the kind, quality, quantity, intended purpose, values..." of goods or services. The proviso allows registration only on acquired distinctiveness. Courts assess composite marks as a whole (Bombay HC and Delhi HC rulings, as reported). In Marico v. Agro Tech (2010), descriptive phrases got no protection. [Casemine analysis](https://www.casemine.com/in/column/analysis-of-section-9(1)(b)-of-the-trade-marks-act,-1999/view); [Mondaq, Delhi HC composite marks](https://www.mondaq.com/india/trademark/1771672/when-description-is-not-disqualification-delhi-high-court-rules-in-favour-of-composite-marks); [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/trademark/grounds-of-refusal-of-a-trademark-in-india/)
- **Memorability:** GrowthBadger found .com URLs recalled correctly 44% of the time vs 33% for .co. [GrowthBadger](https://growthbadger.com/top-level-domains/)
- Mueller recommends a name you can build a brand on and that "can survive" business changes. [SEJ 2024](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/john-mueller-on-keyword-domain-names-and-branding-for-seo/504423/)
- Branded web mentions drive AI citations (Ahrefs Dec 2025, as summarised). [myamazonguy.com summary](https://myamazonguy.com/news/brand-mentions-in-ai-search-platforms/)

### Inferences
- The project's working name, "Tandem After Dark", is suggestive rather than descriptive, so it is trademarkable in principle. "Tandem" alone is a common word with likely crowded classes (Class 9/41), so a clearance search is needed. "After Dark" adds an evocative, non-explicit adult cue that passes 4+ metadata review more plausibly than "spicy" or "naughty".
- Radio test: a name heard once ("tandem dot app", "play tandem dot com") should be spellable without clarification. Avoid homophones, hyphens, numbers and deliberate misspellings.
- A useful pattern: brandable core + optional generic modifier for the domain if the bare .com is taken (e.g. "get", "play", "-game", "-app"). This is a partial-match domain whose keyword part is generic enough not to harm trademark filing on the core word.
- Secure defensive variants: .com, .app, .in, and common typos. Redirect all to one canonical host so brand mentions and links consolidate.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study links brandable vs descriptive domains to word-of-mouth or recall in 2020-2026. Evidence is legal doctrine plus older TLD recall surveys.
- No clearance search for "Tandem" in USPTO or IP India classes 9, 28 and 41 was performed.
