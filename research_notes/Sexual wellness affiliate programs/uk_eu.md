# Sexual wellness affiliate programs and disclosure/privacy rules: UK and EU (as of Nov 2026)

Research context: couples intimacy web app (suggestive, 18+, founder in India) showing optional, clearly labelled affiliate links to blindfolds, massage oils/candles, silk ties, feather ticklers, couples' toys, lubricant. Research done 2026-11-05 with web search only; direct fetches of affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk, wiki.awin.com and www.awin.com failed (hosts outside the session's allowed network), so official Amazon fee schedules and Awin legal pages could NOT be read. Most programme figures below come from third-party aggregators (affi.io, FlexOffers, LinkClicky, Lasso, affpaying) or public Awin merchant-profile snippets. These are often undated and disagree; treat every rate as "verify in the network dashboard before relying on it".

## Q1. Affiliate programmes: network, commission, cookie, countries, non-EU / app publisher acceptance

### Takeaway
Awin is the dominant network for UK/EU sexual wellness retailers (Amorelie, Passage du Desir, Sinful, eis.de, Satisfyer UK, Ann Summers, Lovehoney US/EU listings), with Impact used by Lovehoney (GB/CA/US per one source) and WOW Tech (Womanizer/We-Vibe). Specialist retailers pay roughly 15-30% with 30-45 day cookies, far above brand-direct programmes (We-Vibe/Womanizer reportedly 2-20%) and Amazon. Best-fit shortlist for an EU/UK couples app: Lovehoney (UK + DE/FR/NL storefronts, one brand across markets), Amorelie (DACH), eis.de (DE, high rates), Sinful (Nordics), Passage du Desir (FR/NL/DE). Bondara looks closed; Ann Summers pays little (about 5%).

## Q2. Disclosure rules: UK ASA/CAP + CMA; EU UCPD/DSA; Germany and France adult-product advertising

### Takeaway
UK: affiliate links are advertising under the CAP Code and must be "obviously identifiable"; ASA/CMA prefer plain labels like "Ad"/"Advert" and have ruled "aff" insufficient; both the brand and the affiliate are responsible. EU: undisclosed affiliate links can be a misleading commercial practice under the UCPD; the Digital Fairness Act is still only a proposal (expected late 2026). Germany has a live but rarely enforced administrative offence (section 119(2) OWiG) for offering or promoting sexual-use items publicly in a grossly offensive/harassing way; France's main risk is article 227-24 Code penal (pornographic messages visible to minors), which suggestive product links do not obviously trigger.

### Cited Findings
**UK**
- ASA/CMA Influencers' Guide defines affiliate marketing as content containing a hyperlink or discount code where the publisher is paid per click-through or sale — [ASA Influencers' guide](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/influencers-guide.html)
- Recommended labels: "Ad", "Advert", "Advertising", "Advertisement"; guide advises against "Affiliate"/"aff" and other abbreviations consumers may not understand — [ASA Influencers' guide](https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html); [ASA guidance PDF](https://www.asa.org.uk/static/9cc1fb3f-1288-405d-af3468ff18277299/INFLUENCERGuidanceupdatev6HR.pdf)
- ASA ruling (Sainsbury's affiliate, 2024): label "aff" ruled insufficient — [RPC, Winter 2024](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/winter-2024/influencers-and-affiliate-links-the-whole-marketing-chain-must-know-the-rules/); [DWF round-up Sept 2024](https://dwfgroup.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2024/10/asa-rulings-round-up-25-september-2024)
- Brand and affiliate are both responsible under the CAP Code, even with intermediaries — [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/winter-2024/influencers-and-affiliate-links-the-whole-marketing-chain-must-know-the-rules/)
- Awin (2024) says the ASA accepts #ad for affiliate links — [Awin](https://awin.com/gb/news-and-events/industry-news/affiliate-links-on-instagram-stories)
- The guide is being updated for the unfair commercial practices provisions of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (DMCCA), which replaced the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations; update date not found — [ASA Influencers' guide](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/influencers-guide.html)

**EU**
- Commentators read commission from affiliate links as consideration creating commercial intent; failure to disclose affiliate links may be a misleading commercial practice under the UCPD; the Commission runs an Influencer Legal Hub — [Osborne Clarke on DFA](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/digital-fairness-act-unpacked-social-media-influencers); [Greenberg Traurig, Apr 2025](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/4/influencer-marketing-practices-under-scrutiny-in-europe); [Make Influence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/influencer-marketing-disclosure-rules-denmark-and-eu)
- Digital Fairness Act: still a proposal; expected dates conflict (Q3 2026, Q4 2026, or 11 November 2026) — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/aut/legal-updates/inside-the-eu-s-planned-digital-fairness-act-digital-design-subscription-traps-and-influencer-marketing); [Affiverse](https://www.affiversemedia.com/eus-digital-fairness-act-could-redefine-influencer-and-affiliate-marketing); EASA (June 2026) argues no new legislation needed — [EASA feedback](https://www.easa-alliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/EASA-feedback-DFA-call-for-evidence.pdf)

**Germany**
- Section 119(2) OWiG: administrative offence to offer, announce or promote ("anbietet, ankuendigt, anpreist") items serving sexual use publicly in a manner capable of harassing others or that is grossly offensive (incorporating Abs. 1) — [gesetze-im-internet.de section 119 OWiG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/owig_1968/__119.html)
- Courts have read related ad bans narrowly given changing public attitudes (prostitution ads case, not sex toys); advocacy group bufaS calls for repeal — [bufaS PDF](https://www.bufas.net/cms/wp-content/uploads/WB-Werbeverbot.pdf); [lexetius commentary](https://lexetius.com/OWiG/119)
- Google Merchant policy (not law) notes Germany-specific restrictions on sexualised nudity in ads — [Google Merchant Center policy](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6172256?hl=en-GB)

**France**
- Old article 283 Code penal (outrage aux bonnes moeurs) was replaced in 1994 by article 227-24, which penalises making messages of pornographic character accessible to minors — [Assemblee nationale written question](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/10/questions/QANR5L10QE47550.pdf); [AN question](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/12/questions/QANR5L12QE54129.pdf) (both OLD parliamentary answers)
- Article R624-2 targets unsolicited indecent advertising (e.g. letterbox) — same source, old.

### Inferences
- For the app: put a visible "Ad" / "Advert" label (plus a plain sentence such as "We earn a commission if you buy through these links") on or next to each link, not only in a footer; avoid "aff". This satisfies UK CAP and is the safest reading of UCPD.
- As a trader selling the Couple Pass, the app is clearly acting for commercial purposes; affiliate links are "commercial communications".
- Germany: section 119 is aimed at public, offensive display. Opt-in links behind an 18+ gate, with product images that are not explicit, are very unlikely to be "grossly offensive" public advertising; the bigger German issue is JMStV (below, gap).
- France: product links to massage oil, blindfolds etc. are not pornographic messages; keeping the shop behind the 18+ gate and avoiding explicit imagery addresses 227-24. Note France's SREN law (2024) age-verification regime targets pornographic content providers, not suggestive shops (from general knowledge; not verified here).

### Gaps
- DSA: Article 26 (ad transparency) applies to online platforms showing ads; whether a game web app with first-party affiliate links is an "online platform" under the DSA was not researched with sources. Likely not (it does not host user content for dissemination), so DSA art. 26 probably does not apply; unverified.
- Germany JMStV section 4/5 (developmentally harmful content) and how it applies to online sex-toy ads: no source found in this session. Commonly, sex-toy shops treat non-explicit product ads as acceptable and gate explicit content; verify with a German lawyer.
- CMA guidance specific to affiliate marketing under DMCCA (in force April 2025) and the updated ASA guide date: not confirmed.
- The 2021 Commission UCPD Guidance text (section 4.2 on influencer marketing) was not fetched directly.

## Q3. GDPR and ePrivacy for outbound affiliate clicks

### Takeaway
The ePrivacy cookie rule (Art. 5(3), and UK PECR) attaches to whoever stores or reads on the user's device. If the app sets no cookies and the network/retailer sets the tracking cookie on its own domain after the click, the app is not the party "storing" the cookie, but networks like Awin contractually ask publishers to collect consent and pass a consent signal in the link, while Webgains puts the duty on the merchant. Practitioner sources disagree; there is no regulator guidance found on this exact scenario.

### Cited Findings
- Affiliate tracking cookies are not strictly necessary, so they need opt-in consent before being written (vendor view) — [CookieChimp](https://cookiechimp.com/blog/do-affiliate-cookies-require-consent); [CookieYes](https://www.cookieyes.com/blog/affiliate-cookie-consent-requirements/)
- Awin guidance says publishers should obtain consent for Awin's tracking and convey it via TCF consent parameters added to affiliate links — [Awin wiki: Consent Signals](https://wiki.awin.com/index.php/Consent_Signals) (page itself could not be fetched; content per search summary)
- Webgains places consent collection on advertisers (merchants), who must verify any publisher-collected consent — [Webgains](https://www.webgains.com/public/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/16380)
- Tradedoubler and Daisycon publish GDPR guidance for publishers — [Tradedoubler](https://www.tradedoubler.com/nl/?p=25163); [Daisycon FAQ](https://faq-publisher.daisycon.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000715489-As-a-publisher-what-should-I-do-to-comply-with-the-GDPR)
- ePrivacy Art. 5(3) is the consent trigger; a GDPR lawful basis does not remove the cookie-consent requirement — [CookieYes](https://www.cookieyes.com/blog/affiliate-cookie-consent-requirements/); [GDPR Local](https://gdprlocal.com/gdpr-compliance-for-affiliate-marketing/)

### Inferences
- Minimal-risk design for Tandem (which has no accounts, no game database and a "no third-party requests" rule): render affiliate links as plain `<a href>` with `rel="sponsored noopener noreferrer"` and `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"` (or `strict-origin`) so no game URL, room code or path leaks to the network; never append any game/user identifiers as sub-IDs (`clickref`, `subId`) except a coarse static tag like `shop-map`; load no network pixels, link-wrapper scripts or product-feed images from network CDNs inside the app. Then the app itself stores/reads nothing on the device and does not need a cookie banner for the links.
- The network's redirect domain and the retailer will set cookies on their own domains and run their own consent banners; that is their ePrivacy responsibility, but Awin's publisher terms may still require the publisher to pass a consent signal. A simple approach is to state in `/privacy` that tapping a shop link takes you to a third-party retailer via an affiliate network that may set its own cookies, and to show a one-line notice before leaving.
- A click on a sexual wellness link can reveal sexual-life interest (GDPR Art. 9 special category) to the network/retailer. Not passing identifiers and stripping the referrer keeps the app from disclosing anything beyond "someone clicked from this site". Note: the HTTP Referer header to the network still reveals the originating domain unless `no-referrer` is used; some networks need the referring domain to validate traffic, so `strict-origin` (domain only) may be required by advertiser terms.
- Existing CLAUDE.md rules (no third-party requests, stats are totals only) fit this design: count affiliate clicks only as daily totals if at all.

### Gaps
- No EDPB/ICO guidance found that addresses outbound affiliate links where the publisher sets no cookies; no regulator statement on Referer headers as personal data in affiliate contexts.
- Awin's publisher terms on mandatory consent parameters could not be read directly (domain blocked); confirm whether passing `cons=`/TCF parameters is mandatory or optional for publishers without a CMP.
- UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 relaxations to PECR cookie consent (e.g. analytics exemptions) were not researched; affiliate cookies are likely still outside any exemption.
