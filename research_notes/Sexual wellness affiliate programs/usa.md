# Sexual wellness affiliate programs: United States (and Canada), as of November 2026

Research note on method: this session's network blocked direct fetches of affiliate-program.amazon.com, ftc.gov, conductatlas.com, affi.io and flexoffers.com. Every finding below comes from web-search result snippets. Most are third-party affiliate directories (FlexOffers, affi.io, Shopper.com, LinkClicky, Lasso, Affilitizer) or law-firm summaries, not the retailers' or regulators' own pages. Treat all commission and cookie figures as **unverified and possibly stale**. Confirm each one in the network dashboard (Awin, Impact, CJ, FlexOffers) before relying on it.

## 1. US retailer and brand affiliate programs (network, commission, cookie, publisher eligibility)

### Takeaway
Most US sexual wellness retailers run programs on mainstream networks: Awin (which now includes ShareASale), Impact, CJ and FlexOffers. Directory-reported commissions are mostly 5-20% on sales, with cookies of 7-120 days. Lovehoney US (Awin, 120-day cookie reported) and Dame (Impact, ~15%, 30 days) look like the best fit for a "suggestive, couples" catalogue. No source confirmed whether these programs accept app or game publishers or India-based publishers. That has to be checked during each application.

## 2. Amazon Associates (US): adult-content restrictions, commission, 180-day rule

### Takeaway
Amazon's Program Policies make a site "unsuitable" if it "promote[s] or contain[s] sexually explicit or obscene materials". Amazon can also judge any content inappropriate at its sole discretion. A non-explicit, suggestive couples app is not clearly barred. The risk sits in Amazon's discretion and in product-level exclusions, and nothing found says linking to sexual wellness items from such a site is expressly allowed. Health & Personal Care, where most of these products sit, earns a low rate: 1% in most 2026 guides, with conflicting figures. New accounts must make 3 qualifying sales within 180 days.

### Cited Findings
- Program Policies, as summarised by a third-party policy tracker: "Unsuitable Sites include those that: (a) promote or contain sexually explicit or obscene materials". The original text was not fetched because affiliate-program.amazon.com was blocked. — [ConductAtlas provision CA-P-064458](https://conductatlas.com/platform/amazon-associates/amazon-associates-program-policies/provision/CA-P-064458/prohibition-on-sexually-explicit-site-content/)
- The Operating Agreement has a sole-discretion clause that lets Amazon determine any content inappropriate for program association. — [ConductAtlas Operating Agreement summary](https://conductatlas.com/platform/amazon-associates/amazon-associates-operating-agreement/prohibited-sites-and-content-restrictions/)
- Guides state that Amazon forbids affiliate links on sites that "contain or promote sexually explicit materials". — [Spreadr guide](https://amazon-spreadr-app.tawk.help/article/guide-to-avoid-getting-your-amazon-associate-account-banned)
- Undated anecdote: some product links (romance books) were blocked in SiteStripe as "excluded from the program". This shows that Amazon also excludes individual products. — [The Passionate Reader](https://thepassionatereader.substack.com/p/a-new-modesty-code-for-romance)
- Health & Personal Care commission:
  - 1% in most 2026 guides; Lasso says it was cut from 5% to 1% in April 2020. — [Lasso 2026](https://getlasso.co/amazon-affiliate-commission-rate/); [AzonPress](https://azonpress.com/?p=16058)
  - 3% in other guides. — [Earnifyhub](https://earnifyhub.com/blog/affiliate/amazon-associates-commission-rates-all-categories.php); [Biztoolkit](https://www.biztoolkit.co/post/amazon-associates-affiliate-earnings-in-2026)
  - 4% in one guide. — [Unilink](https://www.unilink.us/blog/amazon-associates-guide-2026)
- 3 qualifying sales within 180 days or the account is closed (you can reapply). Your own purchases don't count. International applicants complete a tax interview rather than a W-9. — [ainfluencer](https://ainfluencer.com/amazon-affiliate-requirements/); [Amazon Associates sign-up guide](https://amazon-affiliate-automatic.readthedocs.io/getting-started/signup-guide/)

### Inferences
- Amazon is a fallback for the generic items (candles, massage oil, silk ties, feather ticklers, blindfolds), not for toys. The low commission and discretionary risk make it a weak fit for toys. Keeping the game's text suggestive, never graphic (already a project rule), is what keeps the app outside the "sexually explicit" category.
- An India-based founder can join Amazon US; the tax interview for non-US applicants is reported. The 180-day, 3-sale rule is a real hurdle for a new, small app.

### Gaps
- **Verbatim official text:** the text of the current US Operating Agreement and Fee Schedule could not be retrieved (domain blocked). The official rate for the relevant categories (Health & Personal Care, Beauty, Home) is unconfirmed.
- **Adult products:** it is unknown whether Amazon pays fees on adult products (sexual wellness items) at all, or lists them as excluded. This needs checking in Associates Central.
- **App links:** whether Amazon allows links inside a web app or PWA game, as opposed to a website, was not checked.

## 4. FTC Endorsement Guides (2023) and the fake-reviews rule

### Takeaway
The FTC's revised Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, June 2023) require affiliate relationships to be disclosed "clearly and conspicuously". That means "difficult to miss (i.e., easily noticeable) and easily understandable by ordinary consumers". The disclosure must sit close to the link, not only in a footer or behind a "more" click. The FTC's Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (effective 21 October 2024) bans fake, AI-generated or undisclosed insider reviews and allows civil penalties for first-time violations.

### Cited Findings
- The 2023 Guides define "clear and conspicuous" as "difficult to miss (i.e., easily noticeable) and easily understandable by ordinary consumers". The updated FAQ, "FTC's Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking", was released alongside the revision with about 40 added questions. — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/an-in-depth-look-at-the-ftcs-updates-2); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/consumer-law/1349688/an-in-depth-look-at-the-ftcs-updates-to-the-endorsement-guidespart-2-of-2); [Gordon Law](https://gordonlaw.com/learn/ftc-endorsement-guidelines/)
- Practitioner guidance on placement:
  - A disclosure only at the bottom of a post is not enough.
  - A "see more" click is not sufficient.
  - Platform built-in tools are not a safe harbour.
  - [Matt McWilliams](https://www.mattmcwilliams.com/how-to-disclose-affiliate-links/); [Termly](https://termly.io/resources/articles/ftc-affiliate-disclosure); [JD Supra](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/updated-ftc-endorsement-guides-3774514)
- Example wording: "This post contains affiliate links. If you click and make a purchase, I earn a commission at no extra cost to you." "May contain affiliate links" is weak when links are in fact present. — [Matt McWilliams](https://www.mattmcwilliams.com/affiliate-disclosure/)
- Fake-reviews rule:
  - Announced 14 August 2024; effective 21 October 2024.
  - Bans fake reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones.
  - Bans undisclosed reviews by company insiders.
  - Bans buying or selling fake social-media indicators.
  - Penalties up to $51,744 per violation (2024 inflation-adjusted figure, adjusted yearly).
  - [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [DWT](https://dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews); [WSGR](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/ftc-issues-final-rule-banning-fake-and-misleading-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials.html)
- A cited figure of $53,088 per post could not be verified against FTC sources. — [JD Supra/ecommerce blog via search](https://referralcandy.com/blog/affiliate-disclosure-what-ecommerce-stores-need-to-know)

### Inferences
For the game:
- Put a short label such as "Affiliate link: we may earn a commission" on or right next to each product link or card, visible before the tap. A footer note or a privacy-page statement alone is not enough.
- Never invent star ratings, "couples love this" quotes or "bestseller" claims. Write any product blurbs as editorial description, not as testimonials.
- Amazon additionally requires its own disclosure wording ("As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases"). This is a widely known requirement but was not verified in this session.

- No FTC guidance specific to in-app or game interfaces was retrieved. Only general digital-disclosure principles ("Disclosures 101", ".com Disclosures") apply by analogy, and those were not fetched.
- The 2025 and 2026 inflation-adjusted penalty amounts were not confirmed.
