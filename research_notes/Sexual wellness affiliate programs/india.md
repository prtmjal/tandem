Method note: research was done on 2026-11-05. Web search worked, but every page fetch failed: the session's network allowlist blocked vcommission.com, affiliate-program.amazon.in, businesstoday.in, nyaaya.org, earnkaro.com, cuelinks.com and livelaw.in. No public affiliate program could be found for IMbesharam, ThatsPersonal, Kaamastra or Manforce, so those would need direct outreach. Adult-category recruitment has historically been hard.

- IMbesharam, ThatsPersonal and Kaamastra would need direct partnership outreach, for example a fixed referral fee or a coupon code.
- Bold Care is mostly men's performance supplements, which fits a couples' "suggestive, never graphic" app poorly and may raise health-claim issues.

## 2. Does Amazon Associates India allow linking from a sexual wellness or adult-adjacent app, and what do the relevant categories pay?

### Takeaway
The Amazon.in Excluded Products page lists no adult or sexual wellness exclusion; it shows "No excluded ASINs at present". The risk is on the publisher side: Amazon can reject sites that "promote or contain sexually explicit materials". A suggestive, non-explicit couples' game is arguably fine, but this is unverified. India rates for Health and Beauty appear to be about 3%, though this comes from an aggregator.

### Cited Findings
- The India agreement defines "Product" as any item sold on the Amazon Site except Excluded Products. The India Excluded Products page lists only products sold on linked third-party sites, Kindle eBooks, and specified ASINs ("No excluded ASINs at present"). The Excluded Products page controls if it conflicts with the agreement. — [Amazon.in Excluded Products](https://affiliate-program.amazon.in/help/operating/exclusions); [Amazon.in Operating Agreement](https://affiliate-program.amazon.in/help/operating/agreement)
- Amazon can reject unsuitable sites, including those that "promote or contain sexually explicit materials". This is reported from the operating agreement through a search summary; the exact India wording was not seen. — [Amazon.in Operating Agreement](https://affiliate-program.amazon.in/help/operating/agreement)
- A Cuelinks aggregator, which is unverified, gives Amazon.in base rates: Baby, Health & Beauty, Pet, Grocery at 2.98%; Beauty, Luxury Beauty, Personal Care Appliances, Toys & Games and others at 3.74%; overall range 0.32% to 19.01%. — [Cuelinks Amazon rates 2026-27](https://www.cuelinks.com/blog/amazon-affiliate-commission-rates-guide/)
- Other blogs say India rates run from about 1% (electronics) to 9% (fashion), and advise checking the official page. — [WsCube Tech](https://www.wscubetech.com/blog/affiliate-marketing-amazon/); [fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/amazon-india-affiliate-commission-rates-2026)
- For comparison, Amazon.com (US) pays 1% on Health & Personal Care and 10% on Luxury Beauty. — [Amazon.com rates](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ); [Geniuslink history](https://geniuslink.com/blog/amazon-affiliate-commission-rates-historical-guide/)
- Amazon treats condoms, lubricants and erotic wellness items as a restricted "adult" category on the seller side; this example is from the UK style guide. This governs sellers, not Associates. — [Amazon UK sexual wellness style guide](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/02/help/style/Styleguide_SexualWellness_UK._CB1198675309_.pdf)

### Inferences
- Massage oils, candles and blindfolds will likely fall under Health & Personal Care or Beauty at about 3%. "Personal massagers" likely fall under Health & Personal Care or Personal Care Appliances at about 3-3.7% (unverified).
- Amazon reviews the publisher's whole property. The app's public landing page, which shows only Level 1-3 cards, is the right thing to submit. Avoid explicit product imagery in the app's link surface.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed a dedicated "sexual wellness" rate line on Amazon.in.
- No source confirmed the India-specific wording of the "sexually explicit" clause, or whether in-app links (as opposed to websites) need separate approval. The official India pages could not be fetched.
- Payout thresholds and methods for India Associates were not verified in this session.

## 3. Is selling and advertising sex toys and adult wellness products legal in India, and what is the risk for an app that links to them?

### Takeaway
No Indian statute expressly bans or permits sex toys. The risk comes from obscenity law (IPC 292, which became BNS 294 from 1 July 2024) and from a 1964 Customs notification banning obscene imports. Bombay HC (March 2024) and Delhi HC (October/November 2025) both held that customs cannot seize "body massagers" because of an imagined sexual use, which is why retailers sell them as "personal massagers". Linking from a non-explicit app to lawful retail listings carries low risk, but there is no case law directly on affiliate linking (this is an inference).

### Cited Findings
- "There is no explicit law in India which prohibits or allows the selling of sex toys in India." Restriction arises only if items are deemed obscene. — [Nyaaya (Feb 2025)](https://nyaaya.org/nyaaya-weekly/what-is-the-law-on-sale-of-sex-toys-in-india/)
- IPC 292-294 are the main obscenity provisions, covering sale, distribution and public display of obscene material. BNS 294 replaced IPC 292 on 1 July 2024. — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/sex-toys-selling-india-legal/); [Treelife](https://treelife.in/?p=815); [IAS Score](https://iasscore.in/current-affairs/legal-framework-governing-pornography-and-sex-toys-in-india)
- Some sources claim imports are prohibited under IPC 292 or the Customs notification; others say no law specifically bans import or sale, but display may be obscene. Sources conflict. — [LawRato](https://lawrato.com/customs-central-excise-legal-advice/are-sex-toys-legal-in-india-what-are-the-rules-and-regulations-to-import-sex-toys-in-india-242163); [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/sex-toys-selling-india-legal/)
- **Bombay HC, March 2024:** the probable or imagined use of an item cannot justify an obscenity-based import ban. The court called the Commissioner's classification of a body massager as an adult sex toy a "figment of the Commissioner's imagination and/or his personal perception", and upheld the CESTAT order. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/bombay-high-court/bombay-high-court-ruling-body-massagers-and-adult-sex-toys-253071); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/are-body-massagers-sex-toys-what-bombay-high-court-said); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/body-massager-classified-adult-sex-toy-bombay-hc.html); [Hindustan Times via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-st-mumbai/20240322/281814288855801)
- **Delhi HC, judgment October 2025, reported 27 November 2025** (petitioner including Techsync):
  - Therapeutic or personal-care massage devices "could not automatically be clubbed with obscene materials like pornographic books or images".
  - The court read the 1964 Customs notification narrowly, relying on the Bombay ruling.
  - It imposed Rs 25,000 costs per petitioner, recoverable from the assistant commissioner's salary.
  - Customs had cited BNS 294, and the case also involved a CDSCO registration dispute.
  - The court called for fixed standards to replace subjective opinions.
  - Sources: [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/massagers-not-toys-delhi-hc-slams-customs-dept-for-harassing-small-firms-on-moral-assumptions-503951-2025-11-27); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/judiciary/massagers-not-sex-toys-delhi-customs-dept-gets-schooled-by-high-court-for-moral-assumptions/2792561); [ThePrint analysis](https://theprint.in/judiciary/morality-massagers-sex-toys-why-delhi-hc-called-for-fixed-standards-to-end-subjective-opinions/2782351); [The Logical Indian](https://thelogicalindian.com/delhi-high-court-fines-customs-for-harassing-firms-over-body-massagers-seized-as-prohibited-sex-toys-imports)
- **Calcutta HC, 2011:** obscenity charges over sex toys were held "not legally sustainable". Separately, Snapdeal was sued over listing vibrators (older story). — [Treelife summary](https://treelife.in/?p=815); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/snapdeal-court-sued-sex-selling-vibrators)
- Online sale is not banned outright, but it involves obscenity, customs and IT Act considerations. — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/sex-toys-selling-india-legal/); [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/india/sale-of-adult-products-in-india/story)
- Mainstream retail exists openly. Durex, Bold Care and Manforce are top sellers on Blinkit, and Durex runs national TV and print campaigns, including a Times of India front cover. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/durex-bold-care-manforce-emerge-as-top-brands-in-indias-sexual-wellness-market-blinkit-50318.htm); [afaqs](https://afaqs.com/news/advertising/durexs-tvc-promotes-couples-to-explore-each-others-bodies-revive-passion-and-enhance-intimacy); [Campaign Brief Asia](https://campaignbriefasia.com/?p=66750)

### Inferences
- Both HC rulings are binding only in their own jurisdictions (they are not Supreme Court precedent) and deal with customs seizures, not domestic advertising. They still show a clear judicial trend against treating massagers as per se obscene.
- IT Act 67 and 67A punish publishing or transmitting obscene or "sexually explicit act or conduct" material electronically. A text link reading "massage oil" or "personal massager", with no explicit imagery, is very unlikely to be "obscene" under the Aveek Sarkar "community standards" test. This is inference only; no source on affiliate-link liability was found.
- The practical risk for the app is low if it does four things: link only to product pages of mainstream Indian retailers (Amazon.in, Nykaa, MyMuse); use neutral product names and generic images; keep everything 18+-gated (the app already requires 18+); and avoid explicit imagery or language. Platform policy is the bigger practical risk: affiliate networks, app stores and payment processors can each refuse adult-adjacent content.
- Sticking to items such as candles, massage oil, blindfolds, silk scarves, feather ticklers and lube avoids most of the grey zone. Couples' toys are the only item with real ambiguity.

### Gaps
- None of the sources found address IT Act 67/67A applied to hyperlinking or affiliate links.
- The Bombay HC case name and bench were not confirmed because the fetch was blocked.
- Whether the Delhi HC ruling was appealed to the Supreme Court is unknown (no information as of November 2026).
- No source covered state-level restrictions or the Indecent Representation of Women Act's relevance; it is probably not relevant for neutral product links.

## 4. What do ASCI influencer guidelines and the Consumer Protection (CCPA) endorsement guidelines require for affiliate disclosure?

### Takeaway
Both regimes require a clear, prominent, upfront label in the same language as the content. "Ad", "Advertisement", "Sponsored", "Collaboration", "Partnership" and "Paid promotion" are the accepted words. A disclosure placed only in a bio, or buried among hashtags or links, does not count. For an app, a visible "Ad - affiliate link" or "Sponsored" label next to the links satisfies the spirit of both.

### Cited Findings
**ASCI influencer guidelines**
- Final version issued 27 May 2021, after a February 2021 draft. — [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/tech-and-auto/tech-news/asci-issue-final-guidelines-for-influencer-promoting-brands); [Business Today draft](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/latest/economy-politics/story/mark-ads-clearly-asci-releases-draft-guidelines-for-social-media-influencers-288958-2021-02-22)
- Approved labels: "Ad", "Advertisement", "Sponsored", "Collaboration", "Partnership", "Employee", "Free gift". One source adds "Affiliate", which is unconfirmed. — [TelecomTalk](https://telecomtalk.info/asci-guidelines-require-mandate-disclosure-content-disclosure/365086/); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/social-media-vs-reality-check-asci-s-new-influencer-guidelines); [Khurana & Khurana](https://old.khuranaandkhurana.com/?p=9702)
- Rules on placement and form:
  - A bio-only disclosure is not acceptable; the label must be on every post.
  - It must not be buried among hashtags or links.
  - Platform disclosure tools are in addition to, not instead of, the creator's own label.
  - Sources: [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/social-media-vs-reality-check-asci-s-new-influencer-guidelines); [Grynow](https://www.grynow.in/blog/asci-guidelines-for-influencer-marketing-in-india.html)
- Binding force is disputed. The guidelines bind ASCI members but are "not binding per se" as law; the CCPA's 2022 rules reinforce them. — [AZB Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/the-regulatory-evolution-of-influencer-advertising-in-india/); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/law-firms/view-point/demystifying-advertising-standards-council-of-india-ethical-advertising-india)
- ASCI enforcement: 302 brands and influencers were found non-compliant between June 2021 and September 2022, including Amazon sellers and Nykaa. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=367350)

**CCPA guidelines**
- The CCPA's Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements for Misleading Advertisements, 2022 were notified on 9 June 2022 under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. — [Khaitan & Co Ergo](https://khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2023-02/Ergo_31012023.pdf); [Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/default.aspx?id=4713)
- "Endorsements Know-hows!" (Department of Consumer Affairs, January 2023) for celebrities, influencers and virtual influencers:
  - Disclosures must be "prominently and clearly displayed".
  - Accepted terms: "advertisement", "ad", "sponsored", "collaboration", "paid promotion", "paid".
  - The disclosure must be in the same language as the endorsement.
  - It must be made separately from platform tools.
  - Sources: [PIB/DoCA PDF](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/sites/default/files/PIB1892527.pdf); [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/government-releases-endorsement-guidelines-for-celebrities-and-influencers); [K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/guidelines-by-ministry-of-consumer-affairs/); [Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/social-welfare/social-awareness/consumer-education/endorsements-know-hows?lgn=en)
- Penalties are disputed. The Know-hows set out no penalties themselves, but CPA 2019 penalties may apply. Under CPA 2019 s.21 these are up to Rs 10 lakh for a first offence and Rs 50 lakh for repeat offences, for misleading ads or endorsements; this figure comes from general knowledge and was not verified in this session. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/social-media/1278226/advertising-in-the-digital-age-endorsement-requirements-for-celebrities-and-influencers); [Factly](https://factly.in/review-government-releases-guidelines-for-celebrities-influencers-and-virtual-influencers-endorsements-know-hows)

### Inferences
- The app is the publisher, not an influencer, but both regimes cover any paid or material-connection promotion. Suggested wording:
  - A heading such as "Ad · Affiliate links".
  - A line such as "We earn a small commission if you buy. It doesn't change your price."
  - The label shown on the link block itself, not only in a privacy or terms page.
  - The label in the same language as the UI (Hindi too, if the UI is localised).
- Avoid health or performance claims about products, such as "boosts stamina" (common with Bold Care-type products). The CCPA misleading-ad rules and the Drugs & Magic Remedies Act would then become relevant.
- This fits CLAUDE.md rule 7 (no guilt or countdown copy): no "limited time" urgency in affiliate blocks either.

### Gaps
- ASCI's 2025-2026 updates (if any) to its influencer or affiliate rules were not found.
- The primary ASCI PDF and CCPA guideline text were not read directly because fetching was blocked; the wording comes from secondary summaries.
- Whether "affiliate" alone is an accepted ASCI label is unconfirmed; use "Ad" or "Sponsored" alongside it to be safe.
