You can build a full date night at home from three or four short games, a snack and a bit of planning. Below are 14 simple games that need almost nothing, a 90-minute plan to string them together, and a shorter version for weeknights when you are both worn out.

## What makes a game work at home

The best games for two people share a few traits. They are quick to explain, they need no special equipment, and they give you something to laugh about or talk about afterwards. They also let either of you stop without fuss.

Aim for a mix: something to get talking, something with a little competition, something to make together and something quiet. That variety is what turns a handful of games into an evening.

## 14 simple games

### Talking games

1. **Three good things.** Each of you names three good things from the day. The other asks one follow-up question about each.
2. **Back-to-back guessing.** Sit with your backs together. One person says a favourite film, food or place, and the other guesses what category it is in.
3. **The one-word check.** Each of you sums up your week in one word, then explains it in two sentences.
4. **Pick a card, any card.** Write ten questions on scraps of paper, fold them and draw one at a time. Our [what to talk about when you run out of things to say](/blog/what-to-talk-about-when-you-run-out-of-things-to-say) has plenty of starters.

### Guessing and quiz games

5. **Your partner's top five.** Guess your partner's five favourite meals, then check. Switch.
6. **Mini quiz.** Write five questions about each other, such as a favourite season or a childhood nickname, and score each other. A longer version lives in the [couples quiz](/blog/couples-quiz-how-well-do-you-know-each-other).
7. **Who's more likely to.** Take turns reading a "who's more likely to" prompt. Point at yourself or your partner on three, together. Try the [who's more likely to questions](/blog/whos-more-likely-to-questions-for-couples) for a ready list.
8. **Guess the price.** Pull up two items online, such as two kinds of kettle, and each guess the price. Closest wins.

### Making games

9. **Build the snack plate.** Each of you picks three things from the cupboard and makes one plate for the other.
10. **One-song playlist.** Each of you picks one song for the other and says why in a sentence.
11. **Draw each other.** Two minutes, no peeking, terrible results guaranteed.
12. **Blanket fort.** It is silly, and it works. Build it, then play the rest of the night inside.

### Quiet games

13. **Gratitude swap.** Write down one thing you appreciate about your partner and hand it over.
14. **Dream list.** Each of you writes three things you would like to do together this year, then you compare.

## A 90-minute plan

Here is one way to link the games into an evening. Adjust the timing to suit you.

1. **0 to 15 minutes: set the scene.** Tidy one corner, choose a drink such as tea or hot chocolate, and put on a playlist. Agree to keep phones away apart from any game you are using.
2. **15 to 30 minutes: warm up.** Play three good things, then the one-word check.
3. **30 to 60 minutes: play.** Pick two guessing games, such as the mini quiz and who's more likely to. This is the laughing part of the night.
4. **60 to 75 minutes: make something.** Do the snack plate or the one-song playlist and enjoy what you made.
5. **75 to 90 minutes: wind down.** Finish with the gratitude swap and the dream list. Say what you would like to repeat next time.

The structure matters more than the exact games. Start easy, build to something playful, then end gently.

## A tired weeknight version (25 minutes)

Some evenings you have almost nothing left. Here is a version that still counts.

- **5 minutes:** make a hot drink and sit together. No screens.
- **10 minutes:** three good things, plus one question each from a scrap of paper.
- **5 minutes:** the one-song playlist. Play your partner's pick.
- **5 minutes:** one line of thanks each, and bed.

If you cannot manage even that, one question over a snack is still a date.

## Using Nod Twice instead

If you would rather not plan, Nod Twice is a free browser game for two partners, each on their own phone. One of you starts a game and sends a link or QR code, and the other joins. There is no download and no account, and both of you confirm you are 18 or older.

Each of you privately picks how far tonight can go, and the game plays at the lower of the two choices. A night is 12 or 16 cards, including questions, guessing games and sealed answers that you both write and then open together. Pass and Next look identical, so passing never needs an explanation. ## Frequently asked questions

**How long should an at home date night last?**
Anywhere from 25 minutes to a few hours. What counts is giving each other your full attention, not the clock.

**What if we run out of ideas halfway through?**
Fall back on the quiet games, like the gratitude swap, or put on a favourite song and talk. A slow ending is fine.

**Do we need any equipment?**
No. Paper, a pen and your phones cover everything above.

**How often should we do this?**
Whatever suits you. Some couples do a weekly short version and a longer evening once a month.
