When you run out of things to talk about with your partner, don't search for a clever topic. Pick a small, specific question, answer it first, and then ask your partner the same one. Taking turns turns a silence into a conversation without anyone having to perform.

Below: why couples go quiet, a simple structure that takes the pressure off, and 30 prompts you can use tonight with nothing but your voices.

## Why couples go quiet

Running dry is common, and it rarely means something is wrong. Usually it is one of these:

- **You already know the daily news.** If you share a home, you have heard about the commute, the inbox and the dishes. The easy topics are used up.
- **Questions feel like a quiz.** "How was your day?" gets "fine", because it is huge and vague.
- **Tiredness.** After a long day, open-ended talk takes energy that neither of you has.
- **Screens.** A phone is always ready to fill a pause, so pauses never get the chance to turn into talk.
- **Nobody wants to go first.** A big question feels like a risk, so both of you wait.

Notice that none of these is about how you feel about each other. They are about habits and energy, and habits can be nudged.

## The structure that helps: tell me, then ask me back

Most conversations stall because nobody knows whose turn it is or how long to talk. A little structure fixes both.

1. **One person asks a small question.** Small means you can answer in under a minute.
2. **The other answers, then asks it back.** "Tell me, then ask me back" means neither of you is interviewing the other.
3. **Listen for one follow-up.** Ask one real follow-up, such as "what made that stick with you?", before moving on.
4. **Swap who starts.** Next question, the other person goes first.
5. **Pass freely.** If a question does not land, say "pass" and take another. A pass is not a rejection.

Because the turns are fixed, nobody has to carry the conversation, and nobody is put on the spot alone. It is also fine to stop after three questions. A short, easy talk beats a long, forced one.

## 30 prompts to restart a conversation

Mix and match. Read one out, or write a few on slips of paper and draw them.

### Easy and light

1. What is a small thing that reliably improves your day?
2. What is the best thing you ate this week?
3. If we had a free Saturday with no plans, how would you want it to go?
4. What song would you pick for a long drive right now?
5. What is something you have been meaning to try for ages?
6. Which room in our home feels the most like you?
7. What was your favourite way to spend a rainy day as a kid?
8. What is a tiny luxury you would never give up?

### Looking back

9. What is a moment from our first year together that still makes you smile?
10. What did you think of me the very first time we met?
11. What is a trip or outing we took that turned out better than expected?
12. Which of our in-jokes is your favourite, and where did it come from?
13. What is something we used to do that you miss?
14. What is something that was hard at the time but you are glad we got through?

### Right now

15. What is taking up the most room in your head this week?
16. What is one thing coming up that you are looking forward to?
17. What is one thing coming up that you are dreading a little?
18. When did you last feel really relaxed, and what was going on?
19. What is one thing I could do this week that would make things easier for you?
20. Is there anything you have been wanting to tell me but have not found the moment for?

### Looking ahead

21. Where would you like us to be in five years, in a rough, sketchy way?
22. What is a place you want to visit with me?
23. What is a skill you would like us to learn together?
24. What should our next big "just because" outing be?
25. What is one habit you would like us to start?
26. What is something you would like to do more of, just the two of us?

### Appreciation

27. What is something I do that you do not say thank you for often enough?
28. What is a quality in me that you noticed early and still like?
29. When do you feel most supported by me?
30. What is one thing about our life together that you would not change?

If you want a clean way to use these, pick any five and take turns. That is a whole evening's conversation.

## Making it easier to be honest

Some of the best questions are the hardest to answer out loud while your partner is watching your face. Two ideas help:

- **Write first, then share.** Both of you jot a one-line answer on your own, then read them out together. Nobody gets to adjust their answer after hearing the other one.
- **Guess before you reveal.** Try to guess your partner's answer to a question, then compare. It is funny when you are wrong and warm when you are right. ## Frequently asked questions

**Is it normal to run out of things to talk about in a relationship?**
Yes. Most couples go through quiet stretches, especially when life is busy or routines are settled. A quiet patch is a prompt to try something new, not a verdict. If it comes with a persistent feeling of distance that worries you, a licensed counsellor is the right person to talk to.

**How do we avoid it feeling like an interview?**
Always answer your own question too. If you ask "what are you looking forward to?", say what you are looking forward to as well. Taking turns keeps it two-sided.

**What if my partner does not like deep questions?**
Start with the light ones. Food, music, plans and small preferences are real conversation, and they build the habit of talking. Let your partner pass anything they like.

**How long should one of these talks last?**
As long as it stays enjoyable. Ten minutes over dinner or on the sofa is plenty.
