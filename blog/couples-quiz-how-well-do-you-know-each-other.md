This couples quiz has 25 questions about how well you know each other, and you can run it on paper or over text with no sign-up. Score it as a team, and the real prize is the conversation about the answers you did not expect.

Quizzes can feel like tests. This one is built the opposite way: nobody passes or fails, and a wrong guess is a good excuse to ask a better question.

## How to run the quiz

You need two sets of answers, so each of you needs a way to write privately. Paper works. So does a notes app, or a text thread where you both type, then hit send at the same time.

1. Each of you answers every question **about yourself**, honestly.
2. Then each of you answers the same question **about your partner**: what do you think they said?
3. Swap and compare, one question at a time.

If you are in different places, send your own answers in one message and your guesses in a second. Neither of you should see the other's until both are sent. The post on [games to play with your partner over text](/blog/games-to-play-with-your-partner-over-text) has more ways to play this way.

## The 25 questions

### Round one: the small stuff

1. What is your ideal way to spend a rainy afternoon?
2. What is a smell that instantly makes you feel at home?
3. Which meal could you eat every week without getting bored?
4. What is something small that reliably cheers you up?
5. Do you prefer a busy weekend or an empty one?
6. Which text message makes you smile the most?

### Round two: the past

7. What is a memory from before we met that shaped you?
8. What is a moment from our time together that you replay in your head?
9. What was the best day of the past year?
10. What is a skill you wish you had learned earlier?
11. Which place has felt like home to you, and why?

### Round three: the present

12. What is on your mind most days right now?
13. What do you feel proud of lately that you have not mentioned?
14. What is something you are looking forward to?
15. What kind of support helps you most on a hard day: advice, company or space?
16. What is a tiny thing I do that you appreciate?

### Round four: the future

17. Where would you like us to travel together?
18. What is one thing you want more of in the next year?
19. What is a tradition you would like us to start?
20. What would your perfect ordinary day look like in five years?

### Round five: us

21. What do you think we are best at as a pair?
22. What is a thing we do together that you would hate to lose?
23. What is something you would like us to try once?
24. Which moment made you feel closest to me?
25. What do you hope I know about you that I might not?

## Scoring as a team

Do not count points against each other. Count them together.

- A guess that matches your partner's answer earns the team **one point**.
- A close guess earns **half a point**.
- A miss earns a **story card**: your partner says a bit more, and you listen.

Add up the points and write the total on the page with the date. Next time, try to beat your own total. A low score is not bad news. It usually means you have just found the questions where you have the most to learn.

Questions 21 to 25 do not have right or wrong guesses. For those, score a point simply for both answering.

## What to talk about afterwards

The quiz is a warm-up for the conversation. Pick the answers that surprised you most and ask follow-ups.

- "What made you say that?"
- "Has that changed since we first met?"
- "Is there a version of that you would like more of?"
- "What would you have liked me to guess?"

Then try one prompt about the two of you: something you want to keep doing, and something you would like to try. If you want a ready-made list for these chats, see [relationship check-in questions](/blog/relationship-check-in-questions) or the post on [what to talk about when you run out of things to say](/blog/what-to-talk-about-when-you-run-out-of-things-to-say).

## Make it your own

After a round, write a second quiz built from your own inside jokes and stories. Questions like "which of us said that line first?" are often better than anything on a list.

You can also use [newlywed game questions](/blog/newlywed-game-questions-for-couples) for a longer guessing session, or [yes, maybe, no lists](/blog/yes-maybe-no-lists-for-couples) when you want to compare what each of you is open to trying.

## The easy way, on two phones

Paper quizzes need a little setup, and answers are easy to peek at. [Nod Twice](/play) is a free browser game for two, each on their own phone. One of you starts a game and sends a link or QR code, and the other joins. No download and no account, and both of you confirm you are 18 or older.

A night is 12 or 16 cards, with questions, guessing games and sealed answers where you both respond and then open them together. Each of you privately picks how far the night can go, and the game plays at the lower choice. Progress stays on your own phones, and there is a mode for couples who are apart on a video call.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does the quiz take?**
About 20 to 30 minutes with talking. You can split it into rounds and do one a day if you like.

**What if one of us does much better than the other?**
Because the score is shared, it does not matter. Treat a gap as a clue about where your partner is easier or harder to read, and ask about it.

**Is it okay to skip a question?**
Yes. Skip any question that does not feel right tonight, no reason needed.

**Can we play with a video call?**
Yes. Send your answers in a message at the same moment, then read them out. The [video call games](/blog/facetime-games-for-couples) post has more ideas.
