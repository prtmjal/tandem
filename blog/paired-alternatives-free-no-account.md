If you want a couples app without a subscription, an account or a download, a browser game played on two phones is the closest fit, and a card deck is the other no-account option. If you want a daily five-minute habit, a daily-question app may still suit you better. This guide compares the three kinds of product fairly, so you can pick the one that matches how you actually spend your time together.

We make one of them, Nod Twice, so we will say plainly where it is not the right choice. Details about other products are general and current as of November 2026; check any app's terms before you commit.

## The three kinds of product

**Daily-question and quiz apps.** Paired is the best-known example. You install an app, you each get a prompt, and you answer it on your own. Many of these apps show your partner's answer only after you have written yours. They are built to be opened a little every day, and they often include quizzes, tips and small ways to send each other a note.

**Physical card decks.** A box of cards on the table. Pick one, read it aloud, answer in turn. There is nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.

**Browser games on two phones.** You each open the same game in a browser, on your own phone, in the same room or on a video call. Nod Twice is one of these. One of you starts a game and sends a link or QR code, and the other joins.

## Daily-question apps: the case for them

For some couples, this is simply the better choice.

- **They build a habit.** They are designed around a short daily session. If you both like a small, regular moment of connection, a daily prompt does that better than a game you play once in a while.
- **They work between evenings.** You can answer on a commute or a lunch break, in different places and time zones, and still feel in touch.

The trade-offs: an account, a download for each of you, and usually a subscription for the full experience. A daily rhythm also works best when you both keep it up. If one of you is busy or not in the mood, the habit can feel like one more thing on a list.

## Card decks: the case for them

A physical deck is wonderful in a way that is easy to underrate.

- **No screens.** Phones stay in the drawer, which many couples want for an evening together.
- **You own it.** There is no account to lose and no company that can change the rules.

The limits are also easy to see. A deck is fixed, so you may meet the same cards again. Everything is said aloud, so nobody can answer privately or quietly skip a card without it being noticed. And the intensity is whatever is printed on the card, not something the two of you set.

## Browser games on two phones: the case for them

This is the kind we built, for a particular situation: an evening together, or a video call, when you want the questions to feel easier to answer than they do out loud.

- **Free, no account, no download.** You open a link. Nod Twice is free to play today. Optional extras may be added later, and the core game stays free to play.
- **Private choices.** Each of you privately picks how far tonight can go, and the game plays at the lower pick. Neither of you sees what the other chose.
- **Variety in a night.** Cards include questions, guessing games, "who's more likely to" (private picks, then both reveal), sealed answers you open together, and a private match list where only ideas you are both open to appear.
- **Easy to say no.** Pass and Next are identical, so skipping a card never needs an explanation.
- **Works apart.** A mode for couples on a video call keeps the same ideas for people in different places. See [long distance relationship games](/blog/long-distance-relationship-games) for more.

For a walk through a whole night, read [how a two-phone couples game works](/blog/how-a-two-phone-couples-game-works). Both of you confirm you are 18 or older, and the server forgets the game when the night ends.

## The honest limits of Nod Twice

Here is what it does not do.

- **English only.**
- **No daily reminders.** It will never nudge you to come back.
- **No accounts, so no history across devices.** Your progress is saved on your own phones, not in the cloud. If you switch phones, it does not follow you automatically.
- **Not a daily streak.** It is built for an evening together or a video call, not for five minutes every morning.
- **Both of you need a phone and a browser** at the same time.

## Which should you pick?

Choose a **daily-question app** if you want a small habit every day, you like seeing each other's answers over time, and an account and a subscription do not bother you.

Choose a **card deck** if you want a screen-free evening, you like holding something real, and you are happy for answers to be spoken aloud and the intensity to be fixed.

Choose a **two-phone browser game** if you want something free, with no account, for an evening or a video call, and you like the idea of private picks and a game that plays at the lower of the two.

You can also mix them: they do different jobs. If you want to try the match-list idea on paper first, [yes, maybe, no lists for couples](/blog/yes-maybe-no-lists-for-couples) explains how it works.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a free alternative to Paired with no account?**

Yes. A physical deck needs no account, and a browser game such as Nod Twice needs neither an account nor a download. They suit evenings rather than a daily habit, so the right choice depends on how you want to spend your time together.

**Do both of us need to install anything?**

Not with a browser game or a deck. In Nod Twice you each open the same game in your own phone's browser. Daily-question apps usually need each partner to install the app and make an account.

**Can we play across distance?**

Yes. Daily-question apps are made for it, a deck works if you each own a copy, and Nod Twice has a mode for couples apart on a video call.

**Are our answers private?**

It depends on the kind. In a deck you answer aloud. In Nod Twice, picks and sealed answers stay private until you both reveal, and the match list shows only ideas you are both open to.
