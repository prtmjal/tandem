Here are two classic games for two: 30 "never have I ever" statements and 40 "this or that" pairs, all clean, light and ready to use tonight. Neither needs props or a big evening, just a phone to read from and a partner who likes a good story.

Both games work for the same reason. They are tiny questions with an easy answer, and the real fun starts with what you say next.

## Part 1: Never have I ever for two

### How to play

1. One of you reads a statement aloud, starting with "Never have I ever..."
2. If you *have* done it, hold up a finger. Or give yourself a point. Either works, as long as you both use the same system.
3. If you did, the other person can say "tell me more." You get to tell the story, or not.
4. Then ask me back: whoever answered turns it around with "what about you?"
5. Swap who reads after each statement.

Some ground rules keep it comfortable:

- **Share only what you want to.** "I'll keep that one" is a complete answer.
- **Anyone can pass a statement.** No reason needed.
- **Be a good listener.** The point is the story, not catching someone out.

### Why it works as a conversation starter

Open questions like "tell me about your life" are hard to answer. A statement is easy: you either have or you have not. That simple yes or no gets you started, and then "tell me more" does the real work.

It also surfaces things you never thought to ask, whether you have been together for years or only months.

### The "guess my pick first" twist

Once you have the basics, try this: before your partner answers, you guess what they will pick. Then they reveal their real answer, and you compare.

1. One of you reads a pair.
2. The *other* answers silently in their head, or on a note.
3. The reader guesses.
4. Reveal together and talk about it.

Matches earn a cheer. Misses are better, because "I was sure you would say mountains" always leads somewhere. This is also how the guessing cards in Nod Twice work: you guess your partner's pick, then reveal and compare.

## Turn either list into a quick game by text or video call

Neither list needs you to be in the same room.

- **By text:** Send one statement or pair at a time and reply whenever you can. For the guessing twist, each of you types your answer, and you send the two messages at the same moment. More ideas are in [games to play with your partner over text](/blog/games-to-play-with-your-partner-over-text).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do we need anything to play never have I ever?**

No. Hold up a finger or give yourself a point, then follow up with "tell me more." That is the whole game.

**How many statements should we do in one go?**

Eight to twelve is plenty. Stop while you are still laughing, and save the rest for another night.

**What if a statement brings up something I would rather not share?**

Say "I'll pass" and move on. Nobody needs a reason, and the game is better when everyone feels free to skip.

**Is this or that good for new couples?**

It is one of the best. The questions are light, easy to answer and easy to build on, so they are a gentle way to learn each other's tastes.
