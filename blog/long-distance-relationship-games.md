Good long distance relationship games are short, a little silly and easy to skip on a tired day. Pick one or two from this list, keep them optional, and let the rest of your time together stay unstructured.

Being apart changes how you share your lives. Games help because they give a call a shape. But a game that becomes a duty is worse than no game, so each idea below comes with a way to keep it relaxed.

## Why games help when you live apart

Calls tend to slide into a status report: what you ate, who you saw, how work went. That is nice, but it can leave you both feeling you talked a lot without saying much.

A small game changes the question. Instead of "what did you do today?" you are suddenly guessing, comparing or inventing something together. It also gives a quiet partner something concrete to answer.

## Games for a video call

- **Guess the room.** One of you describes a room in your home, in three clues. The other guesses which one, then asks for a tour.
- **Taste test.** Each of you buys something unfamiliar from a local shop and rates it out of ten on camera.
- **Same song, different story.** Pick a track you both know and tell each other the first memory it brings up.
- **Build a place.** Take turns adding one detail to an imaginary house you would share, a room at a time. Notice where your choices match.
- **Guess the answer.** Write down your answer to "what would I rescue first from a fire alarm?" and let your partner guess. For more of these, see [the couples quiz: how well do you know each other](/blog/couples-quiz-how-well-do-you-know-each-other).

## Games for a quiet weekday

When schedules clash, a long call is not realistic. These fit into the gaps:

- **Photo of the hour.** Send one photo of whatever is in front of you at lunchtime. No caption allowed. Guess the story later.
- **Voice note postcard.** Record 30 seconds about one thing you noticed today, and trade.
- **A song swap.** Send one track a day for a week. Reply only with a single word.

## Rituals that are not games

Not everything needs points. A ritual is simply something you repeat because it feels good.

1. **The Sunday look-ahead.** Spend ten minutes sharing the week ahead: one thing you are dreading, one thing you are excited about.
2. **A shared list.** Keep a note of places you want to visit together and add to it when you spot something.
3. **Watch together.** Start the same episode at the same time and talk afterwards.
4. **A countdown that is not a countdown.** Share one thing you plan to do together once you meet, without turning it into a ticking clock.

## A free video-call mode to try

Nod Twice is a free browser game for two partners, each on their own phone, and it has a mode for couples apart who are on a video call. There is nothing to download and no account.

One of you starts a game and sends a link or QR code, and the other joins. Each of you confirms you are 18 or older. Then each of you privately picks how far the night can go, and the game plays at the lower of the two choices, so nobody has to ask "is this okay?" out loud.

A night is 12 or 16 cards: questions, guessing games, "who's more likely to" with picks revealed together, sealed answers you open together, and a private match list where each of you rates ideas yes, maybe or no. Only the ideas you are both open to appear. Later levels open only when both of you say you are curious.

Pass and Next are identical buttons. That matters on a call, because you cannot read a face as easily on a small screen, and passing should never need an explanation. ## How to keep it from feeling like homework

- **Let it be optional.** If you skip a week, skip it. Nothing is lost.
- **Keep it short.** Fifteen good minutes beat an hour of effort.
- **Rotate who chooses.** Take turns, so neither of you becomes the entertainment manager.
- **Do not keep score** unless you both like scoring.
- **End on something small.** A sentence about what you liked is enough.
- **Let a good conversation win.** If a question leads somewhere real, abandon the game and follow it.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should we play games when we live apart?**

As often as you both enjoy it. For many couples that is once a week, with lighter things in between. There is no right number.

**What if our time zones make calls hard?**

Use asynchronous ideas, like voice note postcards and photo swaps. They count just as much as a live call.

**What if one of us is not a game person?**

Try rituals instead, like the Sunday look-ahead. Or offer a game with a quick pass option and see how it feels.

**Can a game really help a long distance relationship?**

It will not fix anything. What it can do is make a shared evening easier to start, which is often enough.
