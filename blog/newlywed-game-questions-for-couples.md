To play a newlywed-style game at home, you write down your answer to a question, then guess what your partner wrote, and reveal both together. Thirty questions are below, along with a scoring system that counts as one team total, so you are never competing with each other.

You do not need to be newly married. Any pair who enjoys finding out how well they know each other can play, whether you met last month or have been together for years.

## What you need

- Two pens and two pieces of paper, or two phones with a notes app
- A way to keep answers hidden, such as a folded page or a separate screen
- Ten to twenty minutes

## How a round works

Each question has two parts. First, everyone writes their own true answer. Second, everyone writes what they think their partner's answer is.

Fold both papers over. Reveal one question at a time. Read your own answer first, then your guess.

If your guess matched your partner's real answer, the team earns a point. If it missed, you earn a different kind of point: a story. Ask "tell me more" and listen.

### Scoring as a team

Add up all the matches and put them over the number of questions asked. Then set a team goal for next time, not a target for either of you. Something like "let's beat our own total on our next round."

A few extra rules keep it warm:

- **Near misses count.** If your guess was close enough to make you both laugh, give half a point.
- **No "you should know that."** A miss is information, not an exam failure.
- **Either of you can skip a question** without saying why.
- **Answers can change.** If your partner says "that used to be true", that counts as a good answer.

## 30 questions

### Everyday life

1. What is the first thing your partner reaches for in the morning?
2. What would your partner order if they had to choose a breakfast for tomorrow?
3. Which chore does your partner dislike most?
4. What is your partner's go-to comfort food after a long day?
5. Which room at home does your partner spend the most time in?
6. What does your partner usually watch when they have an hour to spare?
7. What is the most used app on your partner's phone?
8. Which season does your partner like best?

### Memories

9. What do you think your partner remembers best about the first time you met?
10. Which trip would your partner say was the best one you have taken?
11. What was your partner's first impression of you?
12. Which gift from you do you think your partner treasures most?
13. What is your partner's happiest childhood memory, as far as you know?
14. Which song does your partner connect with a good time you shared?

### Likes and dislikes

15. What is your partner's favourite way to spend a free Saturday?
16. What smell does your partner love?
17. What is something your partner is a little afraid of?
18. Which hobby would your partner pick up if time were no problem?
19. What is your partner's favourite compliment to receive?
20. What would your partner say is their best quality?

### Dreams and plans

21. Where would your partner most like to travel next?
22. What does your partner want to be doing five years from now?
23. If your partner won a surprise day off, how would they spend it?
24. What would your partner say is the best thing about the two of you together?
25. What is one thing your partner is quietly proud of this year?

### Just for fun

26. Which famous pair would your partner say is most like the two of you?
27. What would your partner be doing if they were not in their current job or routine?
28. What superpower would your partner choose?
29. What would your partner say is your funniest habit?
30. What do you think your partner will say is their favourite question in this list?

## Change the game for your couple

The best questions come from your own life. After a round or two, swap in questions about your own running jokes, your own trips and the small habits only the two of you know.

If you have been together a long time, try writing a "wrong on purpose" answer and see if your partner spots it. If you are new to each other, stay with the lighter categories first and save the memory questions for later.

## What to talk about after

The scoreboard is not the interesting part. The conversation after a mismatch is.

Pick the two or three answers that surprised you most and ask:

- What made you pick that?
- Has that changed over time?
- Is there something about it I should know?

## Frequently asked questions

**Do we need to be newlyweds to play?**
No. The name is a nod to the classic game show, but any couple can play. Questions about your own history work best for newer couples, and questions about change work well for longer ones.

**How do we stop it from feeling like a test?**
Use a team score, count near misses, and ask for the story behind every miss. The goal is to learn something, not to grade each other.

**Should we write answers or say them out loud?**
Writing is better, because it stops you adjusting your answer after you hear your partner's guess. Notes apps work as well as paper.

**Can we play over a video call?**
Yes. Type answers into a message, send them at the same moment, and read them aloud together. The [video call game ideas](/blog/facetime-games-for-couples) post has more formats.
