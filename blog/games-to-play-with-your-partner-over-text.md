The best games to play over text take under a minute to start and need no setup: one message, one rule, and a reply whenever your partner has a spare moment. Here are twelve, each with example prompts you can paste straight into a chat.

Text games suit weekdays because they fit around meetings, commutes and kids' bedtimes. Nobody has to find an hour. And if a reply takes until evening, that is part of the charm.

## Quick guessing games

### 1. Three guesses

Think of something ordinary, such as a food, a place or a film. Tell your partner the category, and they get three guesses.

*Example: "Category: something I ate last week. Three guesses."*

### 2. Emoji riddle

Describe a movie, a song or a shared memory using only emojis. Your partner decodes it.

*Example: a ship, a door, a drifting plank. Reply with your best guess.*

### 3. Two truths and a fib

Send three statements about your day or week. One is invented.

*Example: "I talked to a stranger on the train. I forgot my keys. I found a pound coin. Which one is the fib?"*

### 4. Guess my rank

Name a list of four or five things, and your partner guesses how you would rank them.

*Example: "Rank these for me: pancakes, toast, cereal, eggs. Mine is secret."*

## Question games

### 5. One-word answers

Ask a question and allow only one word. It is surprisingly revealing.

*Example: "Describe your week in one word. Then ask me."*

### 6. This or that

Send a pair, get a pick. Chain them for as long as the thread feels fun.

*Example: "Sunrise or sunset? Train or road trip? Quiet night or big plans?"*

### 7. The daily three

Each day, one of you sends three short questions and the other answers all three.

*Example: "Best thing you ate today? Song stuck in your head? Something you want to do this weekend?"*

Looking for questions with a bit more depth? Try [relationship check-in questions](/blog/relationship-check-in-questions) when you have a calmer evening.

### 8. Fill in the blank

Send a sentence with a gap.

*Example: "The most underrated thing about autumn is ___."*

## Little creative games

### 9. Story chain

Start a story with one sentence, then trade turns adding a sentence each. Make it as silly as you like.

*Example: "The cat had been missing for three days when a note arrived at the door..."*

### 10. Caption this

Send a photo of something dull, like a mug or a street sign. Your partner writes the most dramatic caption.

### 11. Compliment bingo

Send a small grid of nine things you like about the day, or about each other, and tick them off in replies. Keep it light and honest rather than grand.

### 12. Date night menu

Each of you texts three ideas for a stay-at-home evening. You both vote with a single emoji and the winner is on for Friday. For more ideas, see [games and questions for a night in](/blog/valentines-day-at-home-games-and-questions-for-couples).

## Keeping text games low effort

- **Make replies optional.** A game should never feel like a message you owe.
- **Use time boxes.** "Answer whenever you can today" removes the pressure.
- **Stop while it is fun.** Three rounds is a lovely amount.
- **Take turns starting.** That keeps one of you from carrying the playful side.
- **Say it is okay to pass.** A reply of "pass, long day" is a perfectly good reply.

## When you want more than text

Texting is a nice appetiser, but some evenings you want a proper night together. Nod Twice is a free browser game for two partners, each on their own phone. One of you starts a game and sends a link or QR code, and the other joins. There is no download and no account, and both of you confirm you are 18 or older.

Each of you privately picks how far the night can go, and the game plays at the lower choice. Cards include questions, guessing games, "who's more likely to" (you each pick privately, then both reveal together), sealed answers you open together, and a private match list where only ideas you both said yes or maybe to appear. Pass and Next are identical buttons, so you never have to explain a skip.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a good text game for a busy day?**

Try "this or that" or one-word answers. Each takes seconds to send and seconds to answer.

**How do I start a text game without it feeling forced?**

Keep it tiny and playful. Send one question with no pressure to reply, and let your partner join in when they can.

**Should we play every day?**

Only if you both enjoy it. Some couples like a daily habit, others prefer a message now and then, and both are fine.

**Can text games work in a new relationship?**

Yes. Light games like this or that are easy to answer and easy to pass, which makes them a gentle way to get to know each other.
