Here are 45 "who's more likely to" questions for couples, sorted into funny, sweet, romantic and everyday. The trick that makes the game fun instead of a fight is to pick privately first, then reveal at the same moment.

## How to play so it stays fun

The classic way is to read a prompt aloud and both point. The trouble is that the second person to point can copy or dodge. Whoever points first also sets the tone, and a teasing answer can land harder than it was meant to.

Try this instead:

1. One of you reads a prompt.
2. Each of you decides your answer silently: you, or your partner.
3. Count down from three and reveal together.
4. Say why, in one sentence, before the next prompt.

Matching answers get a small cheer. Mismatched answers are the interesting ones. Two people who both think the other one would cry first at a film have just told each other something nice about how they see each other.

A few ground rules keep it light:

- **Pick the person, not the flaw.** These are teasing guesses, not verdicts.
- **Everyone can pass.** If a prompt lands badly, skip it with no explanation needed.
- **Keep score only if it is silly.** Points for matching answers are fine. Winners and losers are not the point.
- **Laugh first, explain second.** The story behind a pick is usually the best part.

## Make your own

Once you have played a few rounds, writing your own is easy. Start from a real moment: a trip, a running joke, something one of you does every morning. Turn it into "who is more likely to..." and you have a prompt no list could give you.

Mix the moods too. Two funny prompts in a row, then a sweet one, then something romantic, keeps a round from feeling like a quiz.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many prompts should we do in one sitting?**
Ten to fifteen is plenty for most couples. Stop while it is still fun, and save the rest for another evening.

**What if we always pick the same person?**
That is a result in itself. Try prompts about specific moments, such as "who is more likely to remember where we parked", which tend to split the answers more.

**What if a prompt stings a little?**
Skip it. Nobody owes an explanation for passing, and it is fine to say "let's do the next one".

**Can we play this over a video call?**
Yes. Send your picks by message at the count of three, or use a game where each person answers privately on their own screen.
