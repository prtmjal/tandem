A consent-first couples game is one where each person's comfort is built into the rules, so nobody has to ask for it out loud. Choices are private, passing is easy, and the game never shows who held back. When saying no costs nothing, the yeses you do get are more honest.

This post explains what that means in plain English, how it works in practice, and how to use the same idea in any game you play, with or without Nod Twice.

## What "consent-first" means

Most couples games are built around the cards. You draw one, and if you do not want to do it, you have to say so in front of your partner. Saying "not that one" can feel like a small rejection, so many people go along with things they would rather skip.

Consent-first flips the order. Before anything else, the game asks how each of you feels, in a way your partner cannot see. Then it plays within the limits you both set. Comfort comes first, and the fun happens inside it.

Three ideas sit underneath this:

- **Private before public.** You answer before you hear your partner's answer, so you cannot bend yours to match.
- **No is free.** A no needs no reason, no apology and no negotiation.
- **Nobody is singled out.** The game never reveals who said no, so there is nothing to feel awkward about afterwards.

## Why it makes people play more honestly

When a "no" has to be spoken, people weigh it against the mood in the room, the fear of spoiling the evening, the chance of being asked "why not?" Often the easiest move is to say yes and hope for the best.

Remove that cost and the weighing changes. A private answer is just information, not a verdict on your partner. People are more truthful when the truth is safe to give, and that makes a game feel relaxed rather than tense.

Because you both know the game respects limits, a "yes" also means more.

## How Nod Twice puts it into practice

Nod Twice is a free browser game where each of you plays on your own phone. Here is how the consent-first design shows up in a night.

### Each of you picks how far tonight can go

Before the night starts, each person chooses, privately, how far tonight can go. The game plays at the lower of the two picks. Neither of you sees what the other chose, so the lower pick is never traced back to a person.

### Later levels open only when you are both curious

There are 8 levels. The first two are open from the start. The rest open only when you have both privately said you are curious. If only one of you is curious, nothing opens and nothing is announced. Nobody learns who was ready and who was not.

### Some cards ask each of you first

Some cards ask each person privately before anything is shown: yes, maybe or not tonight. If either of you answers "not tonight", the card quietly swaps itself for another one. The game does not say who answered what, so nobody knows who said no. Every private answer plays the same sound and the same animation, whether the card was kept or swapped, so even the reveal gives nothing away.

### Pass and Next are the same button

Every card has two buttons of equal size and weight, side by side: Pass and Next. They look the same and feel the same, and there are no swipe gestures. Passing is not an event, so it never needs an explanation.

### No pressure to perform

There are no streaks to protect, no scores, and no timers that rush you. Any pause or break is fine. The game never uses guilt.

### Both confirm 18+ on their own phone

Each person confirms they are 18 or older on their own phone. One partner cannot confirm for the other.

### Nothing private is shown to your partner

Your partner sees only that you have answered, never what you answered. Private picks, sealed answers and match list answers stay that way. In the match list, you each rate a short list of ideas on your own screen, and the game shows only the ideas you are both open to, shuffled. Everything else is forgotten. The server forgets the game when the night ends, and your progress is saved on your own phones.

## Questions to ask each other before any game

You do not need an app to start a consent-first habit. Take turns, answer in your own words, and let "I would rather not say" be a perfectly good answer.

1. What would make tonight feel easy and fun for you?
2. How fast do you like to go: slow and gentle, or happy to jump in?
3. Is there anything you would like us to skip tonight, without needing to say why?
4. How will we signal that we want to pass, a word, a gesture or a tap?
5. If one of us passes, what would help the other feel fine about it?
6. What makes you feel most relaxed when we play together?
7. Do you want to be asked about something first, or surprised by it?
8. If we stop halfway, what should we do instead?

## What a good pass looks like

A pass is a gift of information. It tells you something about how your partner feels tonight, and it asks nothing of you except to be kind. Here is what receiving one well looks like:

- **Say thanks, or say nothing.** "No problem" is plenty. You do not need a speech.
- **Do not ask why.** If your partner wants to explain, they will. Asking turns a free "no" into a costly one.
- **Move on straight away.** Pick the next card, change the subject.
- **Do not keep score.** One pass tonight says nothing about next week.

A good test: if your partner could pass ten times in a row and still feel warm toward you at the end, the game is doing its job.

## Use the idea anywhere

Consent-first is a design idea, not a product feature. You can use it in any game you play, with or without Nod Twice:

- Write answers privately before sharing them.
- Make "pass" as easy as "go".
- Agree on one small signal that needs no explanation.
- Let the more cautious of the two set the pace.
- Never ask for a reason behind a no.

Other games use the same pattern. In [who's more likely to](/blog/whos-more-likely-to-questions-for-couples), each person picks first and then both reveal together. A regular [relationship check-in](/blog/relationship-check-in-questions) works the same way: ask privately, share on purpose, respond kindly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can my partner see my private choices?**
No. Your partner sees only that you have answered. They never see your picks, and a "not tonight" simply swaps the card without saying who chose it.

**What if we want different things on a given night?**
The game plays at the lower of your two picks, so nobody is pushed past where they are comfortable. Later levels open only when you are both curious, and you can always pass with one tap.

**Does the game keep a record of what we said?**
Private answers are held only while the night is running. The server forgets the game when the night ends, and your progress is saved on your own phones.

**Do we need to download anything?**
No. It is a browser game with no download and no account. One of you starts a game and sends a link or QR code, and each of you confirms you are 18 or older on your own phone.
