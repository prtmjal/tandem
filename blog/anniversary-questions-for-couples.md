The best anniversary questions do two things: they let you enjoy where you have been, and they let you say out loud where you would like to go. Below are 36, in three groups, plus a simple way to turn them into a yearly ritual. They work for a first anniversary or a thirtieth, and for any couple, married or not.

## How to use these questions

Pick one group per year, or a few questions from each. You do not need to answer them all, and you do not need to do them on the day itself. A quiet hour with tea and a favourite snack is plenty.

A few ground rules make it easier:

- Phones away, unless you are using them for the game below.
- Take turns. One person answers fully before the other adds anything.
- Skip any question you do not feel like answering. Nobody needs to explain a skip.
- Listen for the detail. "I loved that" is nice. "I loved that you stayed up to help me" is better.

## Our story: looking back

These are for remembering together. Two people almost never recall the same moment the same way, and that is half the fun.

1. What is your earliest clear memory of me?
2. What did you think I was like before we really knew each other?
3. Which early date would you happily relive?
4. When did you first suspect this might be serious?
5. What is a small moment from our first year that only we would remember?
6. Which trip, big or tiny, are you most glad we took?
7. What is the best thing we ever cooked, built or fixed together?
8. Which song or smell takes you straight back to an earlier chapter of us?
9. When did we laugh the hardest?
10. What was the best surprise either of us has pulled off?
11. Which hard stretch are you proud that we got through?
12. If our story were a short film, which scene would you keep in?

## How we've changed: looking at now

This group is about noticing growth, in yourself and in your partner. Keep it kind and specific.

13. What is something you do now that you would not have done when we met?
14. What have you learned about me that surprised you?
15. Which of my habits have you quietly grown to love?
16. How have we got better at disagreeing?
17. What do you think I have taught you, without meaning to?
18. What is one thing you used to worry about that no longer bothers you?
19. How has our home, or our daily routine, become more "us"?
20. When do you feel most like a team?
21. What is something I do that makes your ordinary days easier?
22. Which part of us has changed that you would like to protect?
23. What is a way you feel I have grown that I might not know you noticed?
24. What would you like to thank me for this year?

## What we want next: looking forward

Here the aim is not to make big promises. It is to find out what each of you is quietly hoping for.

25. What is one thing you would like us to try in the coming year?
26. Which place would you like to visit together next?
27. What is a small ritual you would like us to have every week?
28. How would you like our evenings to feel?
29. What is something you want to learn, and how could I help?
30. What would a really good ordinary Saturday look like in a year?
31. What do you hope we are still doing when we are much older?
32. Is there something you would like more of from me? Ask for it kindly and specifically.
33. What is something you would like us to stop putting off?
34. How can we make more time for friends and family without losing time for each other?
35. What is one goal of yours you would like me to cheer on?
36. What is the one sentence you would like to hear from me on our next anniversary?

## A simple yearly ritual

A ritual only works if it is easy enough to repeat. Here is one that takes about an hour.

1. **Pick a date and keep it light.** Choose your anniversary, or the nearest quiet evening, and put it in both calendars.
2. **Choose your questions.** Each of you picks three from the lists, one from each group.
3. **Write one line each.** At the end, each of you writes one sentence about the year on a piece of paper or in a shared note: something you are grateful for.
4. **Save it.** Keep the lines in a folder, a jar or a notebook. Next year, read last year's before you start.

After a few years, that folder becomes a small history of you two, in your own words. If you want a different kind of look back, our [relationship check-in questions](/blog/relationship-check-in-questions) work well for a mid-year version.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can we use these if we have only been together a few months?**
Yes. Stick to the first group and the last, and treat the "how we've changed" questions as "how I have felt so far".

**What if we disagree about a memory?**
Enjoy it. Take turns telling your version and see what each of you remembers. Nobody has to win.

**What if one of us does not feel like talking about the future?**
Stay with the looking-back questions and leave the rest for another year. A ritual works best when it feels safe.

**Do we have to do this on the exact anniversary?**
No. Pick whatever evening is calm, even a few days either side.
